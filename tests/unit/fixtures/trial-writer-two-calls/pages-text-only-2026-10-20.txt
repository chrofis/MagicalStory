--- Page 1 ---
TEXT:
Lily zipped up her silver space suit and clipped the utility belt around her middle. Out in the courtyard of Kloster Wettingen, the golden leaves rustled over the lawn, and Noah stood by the central tree with his yellow scarf pulled up to his chin. He had moved into the flat next door only the day before.

"Captain Lily reporting," she said, and she saluted him. "Do you want to be on my space crew?"

"What does a crew do?" Noah asked.

"It goes on missions," said Lily. In one hand she held the spare mission badge, which she had been keeping safe for days. In her belt pocket she had a shiny copper button, just in case. "And every crew member gets a badge. Here, this one is yours."

--- Page 2 ---
TEXT:
A black-and-white flash dropped out of the sky above the cloister roof. Lily had barely stretched out her arm when a magpie swept across the lawn and snatched the golden badge right out of her fingers.

"Hey!" she cried. "That's for Noah!"

The magpie beat its wings hard and flapped up over the white wall, the badge glinting in its beak. Lily ran to the edge of the grass, but the bird was already heading towards the town and growing smaller.

Noah pointed. "It's going to the hill, towards the church with the tall white tower. Follow it, Lily. I'll wait here."

Lily tightened her belt and set off at a run, her space boots slapping on the path.

--- Page 3 ---
TEXT:
At the churchyard of Kirche Rohrdorf, Lily stopped by the paved yard to catch her breath. Above her stood the tall white belfry, apart from the church, with its bronze bells in the open frame at the top. On the very highest bar sat the magpie, and the golden badge shone beside its claws.

"Give it back!" Lily called.

The magpie hopped along the bar and only tilted its head. Against the wall of the yard leaned a weathered wooden ladder, one the groundskeeper had left there. Lily took hold of its sides and tugged. It was heavy, but it slid upright and rested against the pillar with a knock.

"Careful," she told herself. "One rung at a time."

--- Page 4 ---
TEXT:
Lily climbed slowly, with both hands tight on the rails and her eyes on each rung before she stepped. Past the fifth rung, she could see the whole yard below. At the twelfth rung the ladder ended, and the belfry bar still stretched far above her head.

She stood on tiptoe and reached as high as she could, but her fingers closed on nothing but air. The magpie stayed where it was and picked at the badge with its beak.

"I can't reach," Lily whispered. The ladder wobbled a little, and she held on tighter. Down on the grass, the wind pushed a few leaves across the path. The mission was going wrong, and the badge seemed to be gone for good.

--- Page 5 ---
TEXT:
Then Lily remembered the whistle. Noah had taught her on the day they met over the garden wall: two short notes, then one long one that went up at the end.

She wedged her boots firmly on the rung and kept one arm hooked around the rail. Then she pulled the shiny copper button from her belt and held it out in the sunlight. She whistled, short, short, long.

The magpie stopped picking and cocked its head. It fluttered down to the top rung, hopped closer and stared at the button. Lily kept still. The bird snatched the button, and the badge slid from its claws and fell straight into her waiting hand. With a flap of its wings, the magpie flew off over the roofs.

--- Page 6 ---
TEXT:
Noah was still waiting beside the cloister garden of Kloster Wettingen when Lily came up the stone path, her cheeks pink from running. Golden leaves had piled against the wall.

"I've got it!" she said, and she held up the badge. "A magpie took it, but I whistled the way you showed me, and it swapped it for my button."

Noah grinned. "You used my whistle!"

Lily opened the pin and fastened the badge onto the front of his warm navy coat, just above the yellow scarf. It shone in the afternoon light.

"Welcome to the crew, Noah," she said. He whistled, short, short, long, and Lily whistled it back.
