## Did the three fixes work?

**One of three took cleanly, one took partly, one backfired.**

- **The hook is named and never explained — took.** The rule/gate half of the a≤3 class fell 6 → 4,
  its lowest of the series, and no hook in round 7 is the thing that decides the outcome (R6 had
  two). R6's worst-on-i example is gone: cell 10's fantasy arm no longer explains why the dragon
  behaves as it does, and it went i=3 → i=4. e rose to 4.75, the best of seven rounds.
- **The want is a child's own want — took partly.** #8 is the clearest win: a nine-year-old who
  wants to stand next to his father at the moment it happens (i 3 → 4), against R6's badge-smuggling
  plot summary. But **cell 5 did not move at all**: arm 1's want is still a slot at a school
  exhibition and arm 2's is still «das Modul neu kalibrieren» — the exact phrase the new RULES line
  names as forbidden. Both still i=3. The rule is in the prompt actually sent; the model does not
  apply it when the theme itself is an engineering setting.
- **The length check backfired, badly.** It produced **review self-talk inside [FINAL]** in both
  arms of cell 6 — a failure shape that has not appeared in any of the seven rounds. #11 ships
  «Überarbeitung nach allen Checks:» followed by the whole idea a second time; #12 ships, inside a
  German text, the English sentence «Nein, wait — the dash is still there. Let me rewrite
  properly.» Asking the reviewer to *name what it cut* moved the cutting out of the review section
  and into the answer. Both scored f=1 and i=2 for that reason alone. Set those two aside and f
  would be 3.90 and i 3.75 — the check did fix the length it was aimed at (no idea runs past five
  sentences, the longest sentence in the round is ~35 words, down from ~45), at the price of a
  worse failure.

**Cell 2 broke apart.** Both arms were one idea in all six previous rounds. Round 7 gives a box of
conkers under a chestnut tree in the Langmatt park and, separately, a boy laying out a line of
conkers and sticks on the station forecourt with half an hour on the clock until the mother's train
arrives. #4 went i=2 → i=4, the largest per-idea gain on the buy axis in the series. Cell 7 is now
the only duplicated pair.

## Regressions

- **Review self-talk inside [FINAL], 2/20 — new, and the worst output shape of the series.**
  Caused by the round-7 length check asking the reviewer to name what it cut. Both instances are in
  cell 6, and one of them is in English inside a German text. This is the one finding in seven
  rounds that would be visible to a paying user as a broken product, and it is a one-line prompt
  cause. **Recommended: revert the "and name what you cut" clause, keep the counting.**
- **#13 regressed to i=2 and d=3** — the same cell-7 arm the R6 child-acts rule had lifted. The
  round-7 wants-rule ("something to reach, to keep, to get into …") reads as a list of transitive
  wants and pushed a one-year-old back into being dressed and pushed outdoors.
- **a flat at 3.15 and b −0.05.** The narrated-event half of the leak rose 7 → 8 while the rule
  half fell 6 → 4; the hook material is still arriving as an event in eight ideas.
- **g flat at 4.30 with the same three offenders as R6** (the Apollo lander, a small child
  underground, a six-year-old alone in the dark). Check 7 is a *place* check applied to the setup;
  in #17 the darkness is in the closing cost sentence, which the check never reads.
- **Cell 7 is now the only duplicated pair** (#13/#14, both the mitten), but it is the pair with the

## The three worst on i in round 7, and why

1. **#11 and #12, cell 6, i=2 both — «ein Ei, fast so gross wie ihr Kopf», «ein Nest aus
   Dinosauriereiern».** Nothing is wrong with either premise: strong hooks, a four-year-old's own
   want (carry the egg back; be the first to see the nest), clean casts, clean peril. They are
   unsellable because the model's own revision talk shipped inside the answer, once in the wrong
   language. Prompt cause, named above, one clause.
2. **#13, cell 7 arm 1, i=2 — «eine Ziege mitten auf dem Pfad».** The hook is there and the child
   is not. She is dressed, put in the pram and pushed outside; the want («das rote Blatt … greifen»)
   appears for the first time in the cost sentence; the obstacle is hypothetical. The one arm in the
   round where both round-6 levers were present in the prompt and neither reached the text.
3. **#9 and #10, cell 5, i=3 both — «ein Gerät, das niemand je gesehen hat», «ein kaputtes
   Antriebsmodul».** Unchanged from round 6, and they were the round-6 target. The wants are still
   a school-exhibition slot and a module calibration, and one hook is the unnamed something the
   contract forbids by name. **Two rounds of prompt rules aimed at this cell have not moved it; the
   next lever is not another rule in the same list.**
