# YOUR TASK

Judge the plan as a reader who sees only these pages. The brief and character details tell you what the book is FOR; they are never evidence that a page works. A motivation that lives only in the character details and never reaches a beat is a fault to fix.

Answer each check, naming pages. Then rewrite only the pages that a check found wrong.

1. Causality — for each page, does its beat follow from the page before? Name the most arbitrary transition.
2. Arc — does the main character want something by page 1, and is their situation different by the last page? Name the page where the change becomes visible. If there is none, say so.
3. Stakes — name the page where the least is at risk. Say what would raise it.
4. Illustratable — name any scene that cannot be one drawn moment: a montage, a passage of time, an internal state with nothing to see, or an empty stage where a beat needs a character present. Also name any scene that is one moment but a HARD one — synchronized hands of two characters on one object, a realization or decision as the visual, an acrobatic/climbing pose with occluded limbs, fine mechanism manipulation, or several characters running parallel unrelated actions instead of reacting to one focal event, or a group with one shared goal split across separate spots with separate mini-actions — they do the same thing in the same place. Rewrite those into their nearest simple tableau (the visible result of the action, or one character holding/showing the object). Name any scene that shows the aftermath of its beat's action rather than the action itself, and any scene that composes the frame — what fills it, how large something looks, where it sits. A shot type is allowed.
5. Repetition — name any two pages whose scenes would produce near-identical pictures (same place, same grouping, same action).
6. Cast & variety — name any scene with more than {MAX_CHARACTERS_PER_SCENE} named characters, or any page where someone forces or blocks something without being in the scene. Do most pages keep to three or fewer named characters, and does the cast vary page to page — the main character on most pages, the others in changing combinations? When the whole cast appears, is it a wide or distant view rather than a close full-cast line-up? Does the book include some framing variety — a characterless establishing view, a distant-small-figures page, a single-character close-up? A close-up beat's moment must work waist-up (holding, reading, reacting) — flag one built on kneeling or floor action. Flag any page separating a figure into the background without the beat requiring the separation — bring them to the group. Name what's missing.
7. Pacing — name the page carrying the most beats and the one carrying the least.
8. Loose threads & unearned turns — name any question a beat explicitly raises about the plot (who took it, why, where it came from) that no later beat answers. Name any object, clue, ally, or rescue that resolves the conflict but appears with no stated origin or in-world licence — a rule, a legend, or an earlier setup. Name any character singled out ("the only one who can", "was waited for", "chosen") with no reason given.
