[GRAMMAR]
GRAM_ANY: This {LANGUAGE} text contains at least one grammar or spelling mistake that a careful editor would correct.
GRAM_ARTICLE_CASE: This {LANGUAGE} text contains an article, adjective ending or preposition with the wrong gender or the wrong case.
GRAM_AGREEMENT: In this {LANGUAGE} text a verb does not agree with its subject, or a pronoun does not agree with the noun it stands for.
GRAM_FOREIGN_WORD: This {LANGUAGE} text contains a word from another language that does not belong there.
GRAM_NONWORD: This {LANGUAGE} text contains a misspelled word or a word that does not exist.
GRAM_TENSE: The narration of this {LANGUAGE} text switches between past tense and present tense.
GRAM_CLEAN: Every sentence of this {LANGUAGE} text is grammatically correct and every word is spelled correctly.

[SLOP]
SLOP_STOCK_WONDER: The text uses a stock phrase of awe or magic, such as "a sense of wonder", "magic filled the air" or "eyes sparkling with wonder".
SLOP_BODY_CLICHE: The text describes a feeling with a cliché of the body, such as a heart swelling, a heart pounding with excitement or warmth spreading through a chest.
SLOP_FORESHADOW: The text uses a narrator's teaser such as "little did they know", "they did not know yet that" or "this was only the beginning".
SLOP_MORAL_SUMMARY: A sentence states the moral or lesson of the story outright, such as "and so they learned that friendship is the greatest treasure".
SLOP_RULE_OF_THREE: The text strings three abstract adjectives or nouns together for rhythm, such as "brave, kind and true" or "laughter, light and love".
SLOP_INTENSIFIER: The text leans on empty intensifiers such as "very", "really", "truly", "incredibly" or "absolutely" where they add nothing.
SLOP_SUDDENLY: The word "suddenly" (or its equivalent in the text's language) is used more than once or where nothing sudden happens.
SLOP_EMOTION_LABEL: The text names a character's feeling flatly ("she felt sad", "he was very happy") instead of showing it through what the character does or says.
SLOP_REPETITIVE_OPENINGS: Three or more sentences in a row begin with the same word or the same name.
SLOP_LESSON_EXPLAIN: The narrator explains why an action was good, kind or important, telling the reader what to think about it.
SLOP_PURPLE_SIMILE: The text stacks decorative similes or metaphors, such as "like a thousand tiny stars" or "as if the whole world held its breath".
SLOP_GENERIC_ENDING: The text closes on a generic warm formula, such as hearts full of love, the stars smiling down or a day they would never forget.
SLOP_PARALLEL_NEGATION: The text uses a "not X, but Y" or "It wasn't just X. It was Y." construction for effect.

[LOGIC]
LOGIC_APPEAR: On page {PAGE}, a person or animal takes part who is not mentioned on any earlier page, and page {PAGE} does not say where they came from.
LOGIC_VANISH: On page {PAGE}, a person or animal who was there before is suddenly gone or absent, and no page says why or how they left.
LOGIC_ANIMAL: On page {PAGE}, an ordinary animal does something its kind cannot do, such as reading, writing, driving or using a phone, and the story has not made it magical.
LOGIC_OBJECT: Page {PAGE} contradicts an earlier page about where an object is, who holds it, or what state it is in.
LOGIC_MOTIVE: On page {PAGE}, a character acts against what they want or what they said they would do, and no page gives a reason.

[ARC]
ARC_PAGE_MATCH: The page text tells the events of this plan line: «{PLAN}»
ARC_PAGE_CONTRADICT: The page text contradicts this plan line: «{PLAN}»
ARC_CAST_BEAT: In this story arc, {NAME} does something that changes what happens in the story.

[SHOT]
SHOT_TYPE: Which camera framing does this illustration brief describe?
