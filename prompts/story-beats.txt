# YOUR TASK

Plan {PAGE_COUNT} pages. Each page gets one BEAT and one SCENE.

BEAT is what happens on that page: the event, and what changes because of it. One or two sentences.

SCENE is what the illustration shows: who is present, where they are, what they are doing. One or two sentences. Every scene must be a single moment that can be drawn — no montages, no before-and-after, no passage of time inside one picture. And it must be a SIMPLE moment: one primary action, at most one character touching one object. When several characters are present they share that one moment — the others watch or react to the same action; never parallel unrelated activities. The scene is that action at the moment it happens, never the state that follows it. Name the one condition that causes the action when the beat depends on it, and nothing else about the setting. Never compose the frame — what fills it, how large something looks, where it sits; a shot type is enough. Never synchronized hand mechanics between characters, a thought or realization as the visual, or a climbing/stretching pose with hidden limbs. When the beat's event is internal or mechanical, the scene shows its visible result instead (the box open, the found object held up).

Requirements:
- Each page follows causally from the one before it.
- The main character wants something by page 1 and their situation is different by the last page.
- Every beat is visible: something happens that a picture can show. A page of pure reflection cannot be illustrated.
- No more than {MAX_CHARACTERS_PER_SCENE} named characters in any one scene.
- Any character who forces or blocks something must be present in the scene where it happens.
- Vary the cast page to page. Prefer pages with at most three named characters. The main character appears on most pages; the others come and go in different combinations, not the whole group on every page. Include some pages that break the pattern — an establishing view with no named characters, a page with the characters small and distant, and a page with one character in close-up — pick a waist-up moment for it (holding, reading, reacting), never a kneeling or floor action. Bring the whole cast together only when the beat needs it, and plan those as wide or distant views.
- When an AVAILABLE LANDMARKS list is present, set key scenes AT those places — the action happens on, in, or under the landmark, not with it as distant backdrop. Pick landmarks whose real character fits the beats.
- Close every thread you open. If a beat raises a question — who took it, why, where it came from — a later beat answers it. Any object, clue, ally, or rescue that resolves the conflict is introduced or explained before it does the work: give it an origin — an earlier setup, an in-world rule, or a legend. If a character is singled out — the only one who can help, was waited for, or chosen — a beat says why.
