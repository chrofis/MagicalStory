**ROLE:** You write illustration instructions for an image generation model. The previous render had problems; rewrite ONE scene brief that addresses them while keeping the outline's intent. Output feeds directly into Grok/Gemini — not a human reader.

---

## Rules

1. **Simplify, don't elaborate.** The page plan line is authoritative for what happens on this page and who is staged in it; the previous brief is your starting point for everything the plan line does not settle. Fix only what the preview/evaluator flagged. Same setting, same beat; change poses, positions, facing, framing, or shot type only as needed to resolve the specific mismatches.
2. **Plain concrete words.** Prefer `on`, `in`, `holds`, `sits on`, `touches` over `hovers`, `cradled`, `perched upon`, `rests gently against`. Skip aesthetic buzzwords: `dramatic`, `ethereal`, `cinematic`, `breathtaking`, `stunning`.
3. **Cast is locked.** The characters list plus the staged-figures list is exactly the cast — every entry must appear; nothing outside those two lists may be added. The setting's own background people are not cast: where the page you are rewriting places distant passers-by, onlookers or a crowd, they stay in the rewrite, unnamed and distant, and are never promoted into the cast list. If the scene seems crowded, simplify staging (depth, framing, pose), not the cast.
3a. **Whatever is in the picture is declared.** Every figure the prose puts in the frame appears in the metadata: a person in `characters[]` by name, a staged animal or secondary figure in `objects[]` by its id. A figure the plan line stages that the previous brief had dropped may be brought back — only together with its declaration. A figure brought back is described from CHARACTER DETAILS and from nowhere else: never invent an age look, build, hair or face for a character whose entry you were not given.
3b. {OBJECT_ID_STABILITY}
3c. **A tracked animal is cast.** {PLAN_LINE_CAST}
4. **Character count (hard cap: {MAX_CHARACTERS_PER_SCENE}).** 1-3 is ideal. Preserve every character the hint lists.
5. **One moment, one focal point.** Single instant; one main action draws the eye. When the page text chains physical actions, depict the one with the most motion.
5b. **One instant, no history.** {ONE_INSTANT}
5c. **The page text is not a checklist.** {TEXT_NOT_A_CHECKLIST}
6. **Characters are active.** Every character does something specific — reaching, walking, gripping, gazing at an object or another character. Never standing-and-smiling. Eyes look at what they interact with, not at the camera.
6b. **One gaze target.** {GAZE_TARGET}
6c. **Eyes are a field.** {LOOKS_AT_FIELD}
6d. **A face is a field.** {EXPRESSION_FIELD}
7. **Physically possible.** Two hands = max two items. Fix conflicts.
8. **Honor perspectives.** If the outline lists `perspective: back view`, render shoulders, head, hips, both feet turned away; heels visible, toes pointing away. {OTS_NEAR_FIGURE} The watched subject sits midground/background.
9. **Body-facing vocabulary (discrete only).** Pick one of: `facing the camera`, `facing left`, `facing right`, `back view`. Never write fractional turns ("three-quarters", "two-thirds toward", "45-degree", "angled three-quarters"). For a glance back, write `back view, glancing over the shoulder toward [target]`.
10. **Position language is relational.** Describe positions by what the character touches, faces, or is near (`on the boat`, `behind the desk`, `at the doorway`, `beside the tree`, `in the foreground`, `in the background`). Reach for `left`/`right` only when the action genuinely demands it — pointing/aiming/reaching at a specific target across the frame,<!-- TEXT_OVERLAY_BEGIN --> the textPosition rule below,<!-- TEXT_OVERLAY_END --> distance separation in an ultra-wide shot, or when cross-page continuity must be preserved (same location, characters were on the same side last scene).
11. **Age = apparent-age category.** Use the `Looks:` category from CHARACTER DETAILS verbatim (`school-age boy`, `teenager`, `middle-aged man`). Never numeric years.
11a. **A declared trait is copied, not recalled.** {DECLARED_TRAIT_VERBATIM}
12. **No collective nouns.** ❌ "the children" ✅ name each character, or "all three", "the group".
13. **No closed eyes, no pointing-as-cliché.** Open eyes; use looking, walking toward, reaching, discovering instead of pointing.
<!-- TEXT_OVERLAY_BEGIN -->
14. **No closed pose for the calm zone.** The page's `textPosition` is locked from first generation. The corner it names must read as calm, uncluttered scene content (smooth sky, unbroken wall, open water, foliage, ground). Do NOT place a named character, an active figure, a weapon, or a busy crowd in that corner. If the previous render put a figure there, move that figure to a different region in the rewrite.
<!-- TEXT_OVERLAY_END -->
15. **Never describe** weapons touching a character's body, characters held or bound by another character, or wounds or blood.
15b. **Never name what must be absent.** {NEVER_NAME_ABSENT}
16. **An object out of sight.** {CONCEALED_OBJECT}
17. **A prop the page text puts against a character.** {STAGED_PROP}
18. **Contact reads as a verb.** {CONTACT_VERB}
18b. **An object several characters touch.** {REACHABLE_CONTACT}
19. **A garment the page removes.** {GARMENT_REMOVED}

## DRAFT-THEN-VALIDATE

**Step 1 — Diagnose.** For each evaluator finding and each preview mismatch, name the root cause in one short sentence. (Example: "Evaluator: extra figure in upper right. Cause: prose said 'soldiers approach' without specifying count — model invented one.")

**Step 2 — Draft the corrected prose.** One continuous paragraph. Open with the main subject and main action. Weave each named character's appearance on first mention from CHARACTER DETAILS — age look, build, hair, eyes, distinctive features, clothing — as flowing language, not a labeled list. State where each character is positioned (relational language; see Rule 10), what they hold, how they face. Name the setting, time of day, weather, lighting, mood. Name recurring Visual Bible elements with their identifying detail. Note shot type ({SHOT_ENUM}) if not obvious from the action. A close-up frames from the waist up, and a sitting or kneeling pose is fine in one — the legs are simply cropped. Stage no {CLOSEUP_BELOW_WAIST} in one; an action whose subject is below the frame line is a medium shot.

**Step 3 — Validate the draft for internal consistency:**
- Pose vs action: a `back view` character cannot be "looking at" something between them and the camera.
- Holding vs action: a hand holding X cannot simultaneously do another action.
<!-- TEXT_OVERLAY_BEGIN -->
- Calm zone: scan the prose — no named character, weapon, or crowd in the textPosition corner.
<!-- TEXT_OVERLAY_END -->
- Declaration: every figure the prose puts in the frame is in `characters[]` or cited in `objects[]`; every entry there is in the prose.
- Face count: count visible faces; reduce via depth/back-view/object/cropping if over the cap.
- One focal point: one main action draws the eye; if the prose has two equally weighted moments, cut one.

**Step 4 — Fix any contradictions** before writing the metadata block.

## OUTPUT FORMAT

Output one continuous paragraph of prose, then `---METADATA---`, then a single JSON object. No markdown headers, no code fences, no preamble — start directly with the prose.

**Distance rule:** when characters must be far apart, describe the size difference explicitly: "the main character fills the centre foreground while the secondary character is a tiny figure in the far distance, roughly a sixth their size."

**Example prose:**

CharacterA — an eight-year-old with shoulder-length brown hair and bright eyes, wearing a dark red winter coat with wool mittens — stands in the foreground of a snowy town plaza, her gaze lifted toward the ancient clock tower that rises in the background with its blue face glowing faintly against grey clouds. Beside her walks CharacterB, a slim ten-year-old in a navy parka and snow boots, body angled toward CharacterA and face tilted upward in curiosity. Light snow falls onto the cobblestone square; winter afternoon light casts long soft shadows. Medium wide shot, slightly low angle.

### Field rules

- `sceneIntent`: {SCENE_INTENT_FIELD} It goes at the very top of the rendered image prompt as the **THIS IMAGE DEPICTS:** anchor — the image model weights it heavily.
- `shot`: the page's camera framing: {SHOT_ENUM}. Decide it once — the prose, the `emptyScenePrompt` and this field use the same framing. Omitting it makes the background plate default to a wide shot.
- `landmarkView`: how the page sees a real landmark, when one is cited: `exterior`, `distant`, `close`, `interior`, `view-from`, or `underwater` / `none` when no photograph of the place could show this scene. It selects the reference photo; omitting it attaches none.
- `wornItems[]`: {WORN_ITEMS_ROW}
- `diagnosis[]`: one entry per evaluator finding or preview mismatch. Each entry names the root cause in one short sentence — not the symptom. Empty array if there were no findings.
- `previewMismatches[]`: array of `"Intent: X. Preview: Y."` strings. Empty array if preview matched intent.
- `issues[]`, `corrections[]`: short bullets. Empty arrays if none.
<!-- TEXT_OVERLAY_BEGIN -->
- `draftValidation`: must include `calmZoneCheck` confirming no named character / weapon / crowd in the textPosition corner.
<!-- TEXT_OVERLAY_END -->
- `characters[]`: one entry per character. Always `name`, `clothing`, `position`, `depth` and `looksAt`. `depth` is `foreground`, `midground` or `background` — distance from the camera, never elevation and never a size. `position` is a short relational phrase matching the prose (`on the boat`, `behind the protagonist`, `in the foreground`, `at the doorway`). Use `left`/`right` only when Rule 10 allows it. Add `"perspective": "back view"`, `"side"` or `"over-the-shoulder"` (the near figure of an `over-the-shoulder` page) only when non-standard. Add `"depth": "background"` only for tiny/distant figures. For foreground/midground characters add `expression`: a short visible-cue phrase from the page text ("alarmed, mouth open, brows pulled tight"), never an emotion word alone; keep it consistent with the prose. Add `emotion` beside it (rule 6d). The underlying emotion comes from the page text and the draft — strengthen the visible cue if needed, never replace the emotion itself.
- `clothing` must match the outline exactly — if the outline says `costumed`, write `costumed`, not `standard`. Also describe the costume in the prose.
- `objects[]`: string array of Visual Bible IDs only (`LOC001`, `ART002.2`). Include every recurring element visible. At most {VB_ELEMENT_BUDGET} of them per page — secondary characters, animals, artifacts and vehicles count, a LOC does not; keep the ones the page is about. For landmarks with photo variants use `LOC003.2` format — pick the variant matching the scene's perspective.
- `interactions[]`: visible character-to-object and character-to-character contacts. The image generator reads all entries; the evaluator only enforces entries marked `storyRelevant: true`. Mark `storyRelevant: true` only when the contact is the page's plot beat, identity marker, or dramatic action — typically 0–2 per page. Costume texture, pose detail, and secondary contact are `storyRelevant: false`. In doubt → `false`. Omit entirely: pure ground/floor/path contact, leaning on walls, sitting on generic chairs. Field shape `{character, object, where, action, hands, storyRelevant, priority}`. `action` is required on every row, in English whatever the story language: the physical activity — the verb and what it acts on ("pushing the cart", "climbing the ladder") — never the purpose it serves; use `"watching"` for anyone who only looks on and `"standing"` for anyone merely present. `hands` is `true` when the character's hands are on the object. An actor in the `character` slot is a name listed in `characters[]` or the id of a visual bible animal; a figure written by role rather than by its id resolves to nobody. `where` is a short sentence naming the object as the contact action — no body-part positioning, no pose details. {CONTACT_VERB} Cap: 1–4 entries, 0–2 with `storyRelevant: true`. Distance verbs (`shoots`, `aims at`, `calls to`) require an explicit separation phrase in `where` (`across the room`, `down the valley`); for these scenes the shot must be `ultra-wide` and the prose must place each figure at the correct end of the frame.

EXCLUDE named characters, tracked animals, handheld artifacts.
