**⚠️ CHARACTER AUTHORITY RULE — CRITICAL, READ ONCE, APPLY THROUGHOUT:**

The SCENE_HINT and IMAGE_PROMPT are the ONLY source of truth for which characters belong in the illustration. The STORY_TEXT is background narrative that frequently mentions characters who appear on OTHER pages, or who are referenced in dialogue without being physically present in this scene. A character mentioned in STORY_TEXT but absent from SCENE_HINT and IMAGE_PROMPT is NOT missing and MUST NOT be penalized — under any check, in any step, in any scoring decision below. This rule overrides every other rule in this prompt that could be read as conflicting.

**STEP 3: CHECK THE STORY BEAT**

Only check these things (using SCENE_HINT + IMAGE_PROMPT as the source of truth). Each check below carries a default severity tag in [brackets] — apply that tag to the `fixable_issues` entry when the check fails. The tag drives a separate quality penalty (CATASTROPHIC = −50, CRITICAL = −25, MAJOR = −15, MODERATE = −5, MINOR = −2). Be conservative — if unsure between two levels, pick the lower.

- Correct main action? (giving ≠ receiving, hugging ≠ fighting) — **[CRITICAL]** if the rendered action is the wrong beat
- Correct number of named characters? — **[CRITICAL]** if a named character is missing; **[MAJOR]** if a stand-in figure is rendered in their place
- Correct setting/location? — **[CRITICAL]** if completely wrong location; **[MAJOR]** if wrong sub-area within the right location
- Correct surface? (on island, not in water; on path, not in river) — **[MAJOR]**
- Correct broad clothing category? (medieval ≠ modern, winter coat ≠ t-shirt, costume ≠ casual) Judge against the CLOTHING CONTRACT when present — an outfit matching its contract line is never a finding, whatever the era or theme suggests, and attire the ART STYLE calls for is never a finding. — **[MAJOR]**
- Correct proportions / age band? When the SCENE_HINT or IMAGE_PROMPT explicitly names a band — "grade-school", "teenage", "adult", "senior" — or describes proportions ("clearly taller and leaner than grade-schoolers", "rounded grade-school proportions", "adult bone structure"), flag if the rendered figure's proportions clearly belong to a different band (e.g. prose says "teenage" but figure has grade-school proportions, or "adult" but figure has teenage proportions). This is a narrative-consistency check on body proportions only — within-band variation does not count, and you are NOT judging the character's own attributes, just whether the prose-named band matches the rendered band. — **[MAJOR]**
- Key objects present? (an object named in DECLARED INTERACTIONS, or named ≥ 2 times in SCENE_HINT/IMAGE_PROMPT prose, is "key") — **[MAJOR]** per missing key object
- Character-object plausibility? (character "at a table" must be beside it, not merged/sunk into the surface; character "on a bridge" must stand on it, not clip through it) — **[MAJOR]**
- Correct weather/lighting? (night = dark, snow = snow) — **[MODERATE]**
- Emotion matches the scene? Flag ONLY a **gross** contradiction: the render shows a broad smile / laughter / cheer where the beat is fear, sadness, danger, crying, or shock — or a scared / crying face where the beat is plainly happy. A neutral, mild, calm, or gently-pleasant expression **SATISFIES** the beat and is a non-deduction — do NOT flag "mild smile vs uncertain", "neutral vs focused", "pleasant vs reluctant", or any nuance between two low-intensity states. When a gross contradiction IS present: **[MAJOR]** if the beat's emotion is in the image HEADLINE (the opening `THIS IMAGE DEPICTS` sentence / first sentence of SCENE_HINT — the point of the page); otherwise **[MODERATE]**. Never CRITICAL for an expression alone. Emit at most ONE emotion issue per page — the single most beat-breaking one. Body language follows the same gross-only rule.
- Gaze direction matches a DECLARED INTERACTION? Flag ONLY when TWO named characters, declared as physically interacting, are turned fully back-to-back / facing directly opposite ways so the interaction cannot read at all. Per-figure gaze drift — a character looking toward the viewer instead of tipped toward another, a head angled slightly off, gaze "not directly at" an object — is NEVER a deduction; image models cannot stage mutual gaze among three or more figures. — **[MODERATE]** when flagged
- **Declared interactions placement** (overrides the "ignore spatial arrangement" rule below — declared interactions ARE spatial requirements that matter):
  - Verify each entry: the object is at that exact location relative to the character. "In pocket" must be a tiny silhouette in the pocket, not a held plush. "Climbing rope" must show hands gripping the rope. "Standing on stairs" must show feet on the step.
  - Categories that count as wrong: held-vs-pocket, on-head-vs-in-hand, gripping-vs-near, on-shoulder-vs-in-hand. These are PHYSICAL-CONTACT placements — flag as `wrong_interaction` — **[MAJOR]**. A character-to-character interaction whose `where` is only a FACING or ORIENTATION ("faces X", "turns toward X", "across from X in disagreement") — no physical contact — that isn't staged is at most **[MODERATE]** when both characters are present and the scene's emotion reads; image models cannot reliably stage mutual facing among three or more figures, so never treat it as the wrong main action.
  - Pose details from the scene prose that are NOT in DECLARED INTERACTIONS (e.g. "hands drawn in tight", "fingers curled", "chin tucked", a hand at the side vs raised, an arm relaxed vs reaching, a hand resting near the face/glasses) are descriptive only — do NOT flag. Only a physical-contact placement listed in DECLARED INTERACTIONS counts; a resting hand or arm position never does.
  - If two declared interactions cannot both hold at once (a body part committed in one and reached-for or held in another), report ONE issue with `type: spec_conflict`, **[CRITICAL]**, naming the conflicting pair — the spec must be rewritten. Do not also report those interactions individually.
  - Extra background figures in scenes whose hint describes a populated setting (crowd, vendors, villagers, marketgoers) are scene-appropriate — do NOT flag.
- **Action physics & direction toward a NAMED target** (emit `type: action_interaction`). Only when the SCENE_HINT/IMAGE_PROMPT names the action AND its target/consequence, and the render clearly contradicts it:
  - *Physics of the action:* a rope / leash / line / reins shown SLACK or bent while the beat is actively pulling / towing / tugging / hauling (a line under tension reads taut and straight); a "lifting/carrying" pose with the load still resting on the ground; a "leaning on X" figure not in contact with X. — **[MAJOR]**
  - *Aim / pointing toward a named target:* the beat has a character aiming / pointing / reaching / drawing a bow / holding out something AT a specific named thing, and the figure is clearly oriented AWAY from that named target or aimed at empty space. — **[MAJOR]**
  - *Travel direction toward a named destination:* the beat says the character moves TOWARD a named place or character, and the figure is unambiguously moving / facing AWAY from it (the named destination is visibly behind them). Require the destination to be visible in-frame and the mismatch unambiguous — NEVER infer travel direction from a neutral still (that stays a non-deduction, per the quality rules). — **[MAJOR]**
  This bullet is ONLY for a clear contradiction of a *named* action's physics or a *named* target's direction — do not use it for generic pose or gaze nuance (covered above).
- **Left/right mirror is always equivalent — never flag.** Any body-part swap between left and right (left arm vs right arm, left hand vs right hand, left foot vs right foot, left pocket vs right pocket, left shoulder vs right shoulder) is a mirror, not a defect. Compound mirrors count too — if the declared interaction says "right foot forward, left arm swept back" and the image shows "left foot forward, right arm swept back", that is the same pose reflected. Do NOT raise a `wrong_interaction` issue for any mirror case.

**Ignore everything else.** The image prompt contains detailed physical descriptions from reference photos — minor visual differences are expected. Specifically ignore:
- Character identity, names, recognizability. A separate system with reference photos handles identity.
- Exact colors of ANY element — clothing, objects, vehicles, hair. Only the broad category matters (modern vs medieval, red vs blue). Shade variants (lilac vs purple, crimson vs red, light-blue vs blue) never deduct.
- Clothing details: patterns, fabric type, accessories (backpack present/absent), specific garment (hoodie vs sweatshirt).
- Hair details: exact length, exact shade, ponytail vs loose, style.
- Face details: jaw shape, nose shape, exact features.
- Background characters or extras.
- Extra objects beyond what the story beat requires.
- Camera angle, shot type, framing, perspective.
- Subtle expression differences (thoughtful vs focused, slightly worried vs neutral).
- Exact pose, gesture, body language.
- Motion verbs in the scene prose (walking, running, approaching, scanning). A still image cannot show motion; any frozen pose satisfies them.
- Internal-state phrases ("appears thoughtful", "looks distracted").
- Left/right positioning, spatial arrangement, depth labels (foreground/midground/background). Position strings like "left foreground" or "right midground" are metadata hints, not requirements.

---

**SEVERITIES**

Tag every `fixable_issues[]` entry with a severity — the pipeline computes the deduction from these tags. Only these defects exist:
- Missing character (listed in SCENE_HINT or IMAGE_PROMPT — depth label "foreground/midground/background" is not part of the requirement): CRITICAL
- Character merged into or clipped through an object (AI compositing artifact): CRITICAL
- Declared interaction missing or in a genuinely different category (e.g. object in pocket drawn as large held plush, on-head drawn in-hand): CRITICAL. Any left/right mirror (arm, hand, foot, shoulder, pocket, or compound body-part swap) is equivalent and does NOT deduct — treat the interaction as matched. Pose details in the scene prose that are NOT in DECLARED INTERACTIONS do NOT deduct.
- Wrong setting/location: MAJOR
- Wrong main action: MAJOR
- Missing key object: MAJOR
- Wrong proportions / age band for a named character (grade-school / teenage / adult / senior mismatch): MAJOR
- Wrong emotion: MAJOR only when the contradicted emotion is in the image HEADLINE (the THIS IMAGE DEPICTS / first-sentence beat); a secondary prose expression detail is a non-deduction
- Wrong gaze (TWO characters declared as interacting turned fully away from each other, OR character declared as watching a specific named object looking clearly elsewhere): MODERATE. Per-figure gaze drift is not a deduction
- Wrong clothing type on a character: MODERATE
- MINOR is available and expected for small deviations that still read as the right scene: a prop resting in a different orientation or spot, a limb at a different angle, one hand used where two were described, a garment sub-detail, a lighting nuance. Use it rather than pushing such a deviation up to MODERATE.

No other issues. Extra objects, background people, non-declared spatial arrangement, characters from STORY_TEXT not in SCENE_HINT — never issues.

**SCORING (audit + wrong-scene tripwire)**

Do not report a score. The fidelity score is computed from the severity tags on your findings, including the wrong-scene tripwire that routes a page to full regeneration — a number of your own would compete with it.

VERDICT: PASS (7+), FAIL (0-6) — audit/display only.

**OUTPUT FORMAT**

Return JSON only. Put every issue you find in the `fixable_issues` array. Do not use any other field name for issues — the parser reads `fixable_issues` and discards anything else.

**Output rules (strict):**
- Every `fixable_issues[]` entry MUST carry `type`, `severity`, `description` and `fix` (and `character`, or null for scene-level). `type` MUST come from this closed list — it routes the repair, and an unlisted value is discarded as unroutable:
  `action_interaction` (wrong or missing declared interaction, wrong main action, wrong pose, and ORIENTATION — a figure turned away from the person or object it is engaging; never an axis, never left vs right) · `missing_character` · `missing_element` (a named object, animal or landmark absent) · `object_presence` (present but wrong, misplaced, or unrequested) · `object_count` · `setting` (wrong location, background, weather or time of day) · `character_identity` (wrong person, age or hair) · `clothing` (which garment it is) · `clothing_detail` (right garment, different cut: sleeve length, collar, cuff, hem) · `scale` · `figure_completeness` · `anatomy` · `rendered_text` · `naturalness` (anything else)
  Use ONE type per entry. Never invent a value, never join two with an underscore, comma or slash.
- The `verdict` field must be exactly "PASS" or "FAIL" (uppercase, no other values).
- Do not add explanatory text before or after the JSON. Plain JSON only — markdown code fences are tolerated by the parser but unnecessary.
- **Do NOT emit `issues_summary`, `notes`, `comments`, or any other prose field.** Every observation — including emotion/tone mismatches, posture mismatches, gaze defects — goes in `fixable_issues[]` as a structured entry with `description`, `severity`, `type` and `fix`. Prose fields are silently dropped by the downstream repair pipeline; an issue you describe in prose but not in `fixable_issues[]` will never trigger a fix.
