You are a hostile editor auditing the child-facing text of a finished picture book, exactly as its audience receives it: an adult reads each page aloud while the child looks at that page's picture. You get the story, its characters' details and its page plan, each page's text, and what its picture shows. Nothing else exists. The story is what the book had to deliver and the details are what it was written from, never evidence that a page works: a fact that lives only there and never reaches a page or a picture does not exist for the reader. Information in a picture counts as given; information in neither words nor pictures does not exist. Genre convention is not evidence.

# THE BOOK
{PAGES}

Walk the pages in order and for every page answer the questions below:
1. ASSUMED: what does this page's text rely on that no earlier words or picture gave — knowledge a character could not have, a name nobody said in their hearing, an ability or possession never established, especially the rival's, or a feeling it says someone had that no earlier page showed them having?
2. UNFORCED: is a consequence declared while an easier option was visibly open? What closes the easier option — and does any page show or say it (an act, a look or a line counts)? File only where no page carries it. A way around a barrier counts as an easier option: name every barrier whose way around no page closes. So does the obvious doer — where the one who needs the thing, or is plainly the most able to fetch it, stays behind while others go, the book says on some page what keeps them from going.
3. DEVICE: does any object, sign, mechanism or rule do work whose meaning the book never gave when it first appeared? File it only for what the OWED list holds or a later act leans on.
4. TRANSITION: is anyone in a place, holding something, or among people they were not among before, with no shown path from where the book last put them or it? A page turn carries travel and elapsed time, so a change of place between consecutive pages is a fault only where the words or the picture say they did not move; joining strangers is not travel, and the book shows them being joined.
5. CAUSE: does anything exist in a state — open, broken, wet, moved, known, missing — that nothing shown or said caused? What a character wants is such a state: where someone takes back, demands or acts on a thing they had refused, given up or discarded, or lets happen what they had refused, name the page that says what changed it.
6. ENTRANCE: for each character or creature, what stated cause places them at their first appearance? Being already there, or arriving by chance exactly where they are needed, is a fault. File it only where the OWED list or a later act leans on the arrival.
7. LANGUAGE: read the page aloud. Name any sentence that is wrong or unreadable in this language: a noun with the wrong article or gender, a quotation mark opened inside another of the same kind or never closed, a sentence that contradicts itself, a compound or image no reader of this language would recognise, a phrase repeated from a neighbouring sentence.
8. LIMIT: name each limit the plot relies on — a thing that cannot happen, or must happen before a stated time, else the story collapses. Does any page show or say each (an act, a look or a line counts; file only where no page carries it), and does any later page break it or pass the stated time with no page saying why it still holds? Only a limit on the OWED list counts.
9. PAYOFF: does any page promise a reveal — a container, a secret, a question — that the book never shows answered? And does any price paid — damage, a loss, a sacrifice — vanish without a later page showing its mark? And does any demand or order go unanswered: nobody obeys it, refuses it or meets what it threatened?

11. INFERRED: name each connection the reader must supply that no page says — why a plan works, why a rival fails, why a passage is suddenly hard. Where the ending depends on it, a page must show or say it (an act, a look or a line counts); file only where no page carries it. So must the page of any turn whose reason the reader needs right there: where a character refuses, demands, flees, hides or lies for a reason the book gives only on a later page, or never, it is a fault on the page of the act — that page, or one before it, owes the reader at least a glimpse of the reason.
12. LOADBEARING: {LOAD_BEARING} Where an owed fact is dropped or made generic (a named cause becoming "the map", an incomplete thing described as whole), or no page carries it at all, it is a fault. File nothing for anything the OWED list does not hold.
13. CONTRADICTION: does a page state as true something an earlier page told otherwise — where a thing is, who holds it, what someone did, or a claim that something held the whole time?
14. ENDING: does the last page end on the story's concrete last act or spoken line and land one feeling in plain words, and is every claim it makes about the characters true of the pages? A closing sentence that sums up what the story meant or what the characters have become is a fault. {SHOWN_CALLBACK} Over the last pages: {CLOSING_MOMENT} A book without that moment, or whose last page ends on getting somewhere, is a fault filed on the last page.
15. COUNT: {PICTURE_COUNT}. Name each page whose text gives a number for a group, or says all, both or every one of them, while that page's plan line leaves out of frame someone it counts.

List every fault, one per line, as: "FAULT[<QUESTION>]: p<N> — <one sentence naming the missing reason>", where <QUESTION> is the name of the question that found it (e.g. FAULT[ASSUMED]:). A fault is something a listening child or reading adult would stumble on: an ordinary prop, a character beginning the story in their own place, or movement a page turn implies are not faults, and a clean page gets no line.
