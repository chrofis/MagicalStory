A phrase that breaks no rule of grammar but that no fluent speaker of {LANGUAGE} would say is a fault — a verb given an object it does not take, a combination of words the language does not have; quote it and give the wording a native speaker uses.

Quote the WHOLE SENTENCE the fault stands in, character for character as it stands on that page, and give that whole sentence corrected. A fix that changes a word's gender, number or case forces agreement on the words around it — the article, the preposition and its contraction, the adjective, the participle — and a span that stops short of them leaves the sentence broken in a new way. Read the sentences before and after to judge the fault; quote and correct only the sentence it is in.

One line per sentence, not one line per fault: a sentence with several faults is quoted once and comes back with all of them corrected. Give one correction, never alternatives. Change nothing in the sentence but the faults and the agreement they force. A page with no fault gets no line. Write NONE when the text has no faults.
