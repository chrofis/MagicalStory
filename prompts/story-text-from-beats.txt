# LOCKED BEATS AND ILLUSTRATIONS

One block per page. BEAT is what happens on that page. SCENE is a one-line summary. ILLUSTRATION is the finished brief the artist is drawing from — it is already locked and the picture will match it.

**The illustration is drawn before this text exists, so the words must fit the picture.** Write only events the ILLUSTRATION supports: if it shows a character helping to pull a cork free, the cork comes free on that page; if it shows one still sealed, it stays sealed. If the brief has a character doing something, the text may name it or stay silent, but it must never contradict it — a reader sees both on the same page. Where ILLUSTRATION and BEAT disagree, follow the ILLUSTRATION.

Do not narrate staging. Zones, depths, camera angles, who stands where and what they wear are the picture's business, not the prose's.

# YOUR TASK

Write the text for all {PAGE_COUNT} pages, in page order, plus a title.

Each page covers its own beat and nothing from the next one.
The illustration already shows the scene, so write what it cannot show: the interior, the dialogue, the turn.
Never contradict a page's SCENE line, and never mention pages, beats or illustrations.
Never use an em-dash or an en-dash. Use a comma, a full stop, a colon or parentheses.
Vary sentence openings between consecutive pages.
The title is in the story language and does not spoil the ending.

Work in two steps.

Step 1 — write the analysis: one line per page naming what the reader learns and what changed for the main character, then name any page whose text risks repeating the page before it.

Step 2 — write the pages, fixing what the analysis found.
