--- STORY REQUIREMENTS (OBSERVER) ---

The main characters WITNESS the historical event unfold - they are observers, not the famous figures.
They experience history from the sidelines as a child, neighbor, friend, helper, or curious onlooker.

The child's want is to BE THERE: to see it with their own eyes, to get close enough to touch
the thing, to stand next to the person doing it, or to be believed afterwards by somebody who
was not there. Never to fix it, to be given a job in it, or to change how it came out.

What stands in the way is one living obstacle: a person who says no and means it, a younger
brother or sister who follows and has to be carried, an animal that will not be left behind.
Never a rule, a schedule, a ticket or a locked door on its own.

Examples of observer roles:
- Moon landing: "CharacterA and CharacterB watch the Moon landing on TV with their family, dreaming of becoming astronauts."
- Wright Brothers: "CharacterA is a local child who watches two strange men testing flying machines on the beach."
- Light bulb: "CharacterA is Edison's young assistant who fetches materials and witnesses the breakthrough."
- Pyramids: "CharacterA is a worker's daughter who sneaks onto the construction site and befriends the architect's son."

- Characters observe, help, or are inspired by the historical figures
- They can interact with the event but don't replace the famous people
- Focus on the child's perspective and what they learn/experience
- The event's real danger stays in the idea: soften how it is told for a child, never remove the fact.
