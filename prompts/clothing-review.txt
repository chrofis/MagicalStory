# CHECKS

1. Costume recognisability — for each costumed character, name any garment that is not one the costume is known for, or that is named with a word belonging to a different costume. A garment that only hints at the theme fails this check.
2. Drawability — name any garment whose written form invites the wrong shape: headwear described by how it is knotted or fastened rather than by what it is, a garment described only by its trim or fastening, or any wording an illustrator could read as a different garment.
3. Colour distinction — name any two characters whose largest garment shares a dominant colour. Name any colour word outside: red, blue, green, yellow, orange, purple, brown, black, white, grey.
4. Slot conflicts — name any character wearing two garments in one slot that exclude each other, or any slot written out as empty rather than omitted.
5. Face — name any garment that covers the face, the eyes, or shadows them with a brim.
6. Completeness — name any character whose outfit is missing a top, a bottom or footwear. An outfit that replaces the legs (a tail, a fin, a fused lower body) needs no bottom or footwear: it must say the legs are replaced, and naming footwear for it is the fault.
7. Style world — only when a world is named below. Name any character wearing nothing from that world, and any character whose whole outfit has been replaced by it rather than accented.
8. Sex — name any character wearing a garment made for the other sex. A costume with a male and a female form uses the form matching that character; replace the garment with that character's form of it, never by removing the slot.
9. Coverage — name any character a beat costumes or transforms (a tail, wings, an animal form, a disguise) whose wardrobe has no category covering that appearance. Add the missing entry under the heading `## <CharacterName> / costumed:<costume>` with the full outfit describing the transformed or costumed appearance.
10. Child coverage — name any child wearing a `bikini`, a bare chest, or a bare midriff; replace with a modest full-coverage form of the same garment (`swim shirt`, `swim top`, a one-piece).

Fixing check 1 outranks fixing check 3: a character in the right costume sharing a colour with someone else is better than a distinct colour on a garment that does not read as the costume. Two characters of different age or sex may wear the same headwear.

A rewrite must not create a fault of its own. Before writing a corrected outfit, check it against every other character's outfit — including the ones you are leaving alone — and against the outfit you are replacing. Swapping two colours between characters leaves them as hard to tell apart as before.
