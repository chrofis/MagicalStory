# TODDLER MODE

The child this book is for is three or younger. Write for that child. These rules override any
instruction elsewhere to build a conflict, a tension or a challenge.

**A new thing on every page.** The book is a string of small moments, never one situation stretched
across the book: on every page something is seen, met, heard, touched or done that no earlier page
had. Never carry one want from page to page — wanting a thing, still wanting it, then getting it is
three wasted pages.

**The child's traits are the plan.** Give each trait a page of its own, shown the way a child this
age shows it — brave is patting something big, strong is dragging something heavy for them,
adventurous is climbing up to look over, clever is naming and matching, cheerful is laughing and
clapping. A "loves X" detail earns one page, never the whole book.

**Plenty happens; nothing goes wrong.** The interest comes from variety, not from a problem. No
obstacle, no setback, no danger, nothing lost, nobody unkind, nothing frightening or sad. If
something small does not work at first, it comes right on the same page.

**What the child does.** Make-believe doing is the point: standing at the wheel, holding a rope,
waving at a bird, splashing, feeding, patting, pointing, naming, carrying, giving, hiding, falling
over and getting up again. Never anything that turns on working something out — reading, following
clues, making a plan, solving a puzzle, or deciding for others.

**No real-world errands alone.** No stations, tickets, traffic, shops or crowds the child gets
through by themselves. Inside the story's own make-believe world they may sail, ride, fly or be
carried anywhere at all.

**Topic.** A place full of things to look at, and a child let loose in it. Never a quest, a search,
a rescue, a secret or a prize. A costume or a favourite theme is what the child wears and what the
pages are full of, never a mission they carry out.

**Food.** Only ordinary food a child of this age eats, named plainly. Never sweets or ice cream, and
never anything a small child cannot chew or swallow safely.

**Nothing appears from nowhere.** A thing is referred to as known only after a page has shown it
there. Nothing valuable, exciting or edible is simply found.

**Ending.** Home, a meal or sleep. Not a triumph, not a reward, not a lesson.
