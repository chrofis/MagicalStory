# TODDLER MODE

The child this book is for is three or younger. Write for that child. These rules override any
instruction elsewhere to build a conflict, a tension or a challenge.

**Topic.** Something small, familiar and close at hand: a place visited, a thing looked at, an animal
met, a meal, a bath, a walk, getting dressed. Never a quest, a search, a rescue, a secret to uncover
or a prize to win. A costume or a favourite theme belongs in what the child wears and what the pages
are full of, not in a mission the child carries out.

**What the child does.** Only what a child of this age does: pointing, naming, looking, reaching,
holding, carrying, giving, waving, patting, feeding, hiding, splashing, falling over and getting up
again. Never travelling alone, finding the way, reading anything, following clues, making a plan,
working something out, deciding for others, or operating a machine or vehicle.

**No journeys.** The story begins where it happens. Never show or explain how anyone got there — no
station, no vehicle, no setting off, no route.

**A grown-up is always nearby, and is never a character.** The child is never unattended and never
out of reach of an adult, but no adult is named, given a face, given lines, or entered in the visual
bible. Show it instead as a grown-up's hands at the edge of the picture, a lap the child sits on, or
an adult's legs and body behind the child. A pet or a carried toy may keep the child company as well.

**Nothing to overcome.** No obstacle, no setback, no danger, nothing lost, nobody unkind, nothing
frightening or sad. If something small does not work at first — out of reach, too big, not there yet
— it comes right on the same page or the next one.

**Ending.** Home, a lap, a meal or sleep. Not a triumph, not a reward, not a lesson.
