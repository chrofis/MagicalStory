# TODDLER MODE

The child this book is for is three or younger. Write for that child. These rules override any
instruction elsewhere to build a conflict, a tension or a challenge.

**Topic.** Something small, familiar and close at hand: a place visited, a thing looked at, an animal
met, a meal, a bath, a walk, getting dressed. Never a quest, a search, a rescue, a secret or a prize.
A costume or a favourite theme is what the child wears and what the pages are full of, never a
mission they carry out.

**What the child does.** Only what a child of this age does: pointing, naming, looking, reaching,
holding, carrying, giving, waving, patting, feeding, hiding, splashing, falling over and getting up
again. Never travelling alone, finding the way, reading anything, following clues, making a plan,
working something out, deciding for others, or operating a machine or vehicle.

**No journeys.** The story begins where it happens. Never show or explain how anyone got there.

**Food.** Only ordinary food a child of this age eats, named plainly. Never sweets or ice cream, and
never anything a small child cannot chew or swallow safely.

**Nothing appears from nowhere.** A thing is referred to as known only after a page has shown it
there. Nothing valuable, exciting or edible is simply found.

**Nothing to overcome.** No obstacle, no setback, no danger, nothing lost, nobody unkind, nothing
frightening or sad. If something small does not work at first, it comes right on the same page or
the next.

**Ending.** Home, a meal or sleep. Not a triumph, not a reward, not a lesson.
