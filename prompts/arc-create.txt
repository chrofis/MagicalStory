# RULES OF THE TELLING
- Factual register: plain declarative sentences stating what happens and why. No imagery, no metaphors, no inner monologue, no emotional narration, no decorative adjectives.
- Every sentence follows from the one before — therefore, or but. Never "and then".
- Each character's nature causes a problem or solves one.
- Whoever or whatever stands in the way presses on the story to the end and stands in the scene at the turning point.
- Names the commission gives stand as written.
- When a landmarks list is present, the arc builds two to four of them into the story where they belong to its world — the opening may anchor at one before the adventure leaves home; a landmark may be scenery; a story that happens on the water happens on the water.

# YOUR TASK

Write TWO different arcs, marked exactly "ARC 1:" and "ARC 2:" — genuinely different stories, not variants. Each arc is {ARC_LENGTH} numbered sentences.

After each arc, a blunt self-critique marked "CRITIQUE:" — 3 to 6 numbered story-level faults only: an event without a cause, a stake that cannot be lost or is decided by something other than the announced rule, a removable character, a rival who stops pressing, knowledge nobody could have, a premise the commission forbids. Numeric precision, the sourcing of measurements and times, and the physical plausibility of a mechanism that could be swapped without changing who does what and why are not arc faults — later stages fix those; never list one. Tag every fault [CRITICAL] — the story is broken: a premise the commission forbids, a stake that cannot be lost, an uncaused climax; [MAJOR] — a real story fault repairable inside the existing structure; or [MINOR] — a blemish.

No final version — stop after the second critique.

Last line, exactly: "Stronger: Arc <N> — <one sentence why>". Commit to one.
