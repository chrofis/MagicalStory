# RULES OF THE TELLING
- Factual register: plain declarative sentences stating what happens and why. No imagery, no metaphors, no inner monologue, no emotional narration, no decorative adjectives.
- Every sentence follows from the one before — therefore, or but. Never "and then".
- Each character's nature causes a problem or solves one.
- Whoever or whatever stands in the way presses on the story to the end and stands in the scene at the turning point.
- Names the commission gives stand as written.
- When a landmarks list is present, landmarks join the story only where they belong to the world the commission names — a story set elsewhere keeps its setting, and the landmarks appear at most on the opening page before the adventure leaves home, or not at all. Never relocate the commission's world to reach a landmark.

# YOUR TASK

Write TWO different arcs, marked exactly "ARC 1:" and "ARC 2:" — genuinely different stories, not variants. Each arc is {ARC_LENGTH} numbered sentences.

After each arc, a blunt self-critique marked "CRITIQUE:", written by answering four questions as an eight-year-old listener: Where does the story lose them — a moment of confusion, boredom or disbelief they would notice? Is it logical — does each thing follow from what came before? Is it full of suspense — is there a question they need answered, and is the outcome in doubt to the end? Are the figures people a child likes, roots for, and can tell apart? Report 3 to 6 numbered story-level faults found by these questions. Faults usually look like: an event without a cause, a stake that cannot be lost or that never bites (announced but never felt), a removable character, a rival who stops pressing, knowledge nobody could have, a premise the commission forbids, a character who is anyone — nothing they do comes from who they are, an interaction no real person would have — a reaction the plot needs but the person would not give. The last two are MAJOR by default; a stated trait that reverses under trivial pressure, at no cost, is MAJOR — never MINOR; a main cast of interchangeable figures is CRITICAL. That nothing enforces an announced consequence is not a fault; that the consequence is never felt is. Numeric precision, the sourcing of measurements and times, and the physical plausibility of a mechanism that could be swapped without changing who does what and why are not arc faults — later stages fix those; never list one. Tag every fault [CRITICAL] — the story is broken: a premise the commission forbids, a stake that cannot be lost, an uncaused climax; [MAJOR] — a real story fault repairable inside the existing structure; or [MINOR] — a blemish.

No final version — stop after the second critique.
