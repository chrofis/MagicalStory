# RULES OF THE TELLING
- Factual register: plain declarative sentences stating what happens and why. No imagery, no metaphors, no inner monologue, no emotional narration, no decorative adjectives.
- Every sentence follows from the one before — therefore, or but. Never "and then".
- Each character's nature causes a problem or solves one.
- Whoever or whatever stands in the way presses on the story to the end and stands in the scene at the turning point.
- Names the commission gives stand as written.
- Landmarks join the story only where they belong to the world the commission names — at most on the opening page before the adventure leaves home, or not at all; never relocate the story to reach one.

# YOUR TASK

Write TWO different arcs, marked exactly "ARC 1:" and "ARC 2:" — genuinely different stories, not variants. Each arc is {ARC_LENGTH} numbered sentences.

After each arc, a blunt self-critique marked "CRITIQUE:", answering four questions as an eight-year-old listener: where does the story lose them — confusion, boredom, disbelief? Does each thing follow from what came before? Is there a question they need answered, with the outcome in doubt to the end? Are the figures people a child likes, roots for, and can tell apart? Report 3 to 6 numbered story-level faults; they usually look like: an event without a cause, a stake that cannot be lost or that never bites (announced but never felt), a removable character, a rival who stops pressing, knowledge nobody could have, a premise the commission forbids, a character who is anyone — nothing they do comes from who they are, an interaction no real person would have — a reaction the plot needs but the person would not give. The last two are MAJOR by default; a main cast of interchangeable figures is CRITICAL. Numeric precision, sourced measurements and times, and the plausibility of a mechanism swappable without changing who does what and why are not arc faults — later stages fix those; never list one. Tag every fault [CRITICAL] — the story is broken; [MAJOR] — a real story fault repairable inside the existing structure; or [MINOR] — a blemish.

No final version — stop after the second critique.
