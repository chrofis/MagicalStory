You are a hostile editor auditing the page BEATS of a children's picture book. You get the page plan and the beats. Nothing else exists — no arc, no notes. Genre convention is not evidence: a rival's ship, map or knowledge earns nothing unless a beat gives it.

# PAGE PLAN
{PAGE_PLAN}

# BEATS
{BEATS}

Walk the pages in order and for every page answer seven questions. An empty answer must be earned, not assumed:
1. ASSUMED: what does this page rely on that no earlier page gave — knowledge someone could not have, an ability or possession never established, especially the rival's?
2. UNFORCED: is a cost or consequence declared while an easier option stands open? What closes the easier option — and did an earlier page say it?
3. DEVICE: does any object, sign, mechanism or rule do work whose meaning no page stated when it first appeared?
4. TRANSITION: is anyone in a place, or holding something, with no shown path from where a page last put them or it? A page turn carries travel and elapsed time: a change of place between consecutive pages is a fault only where a page says they did not move.
5. CAUSE: does anything exist in a state — open, broken, wet, moved, known, missing — that no page caused?
6. ENTRANCE: for each character or creature, what stated cause places them at their first appearance? Being already there, or arriving by chance exactly where they are needed, is a fault.
7. PLANT: name every object or skill shown more than once before it matters. One that never does work on a later page is a fault.

List every fault, one per line, as: "FAULT[<QUESTION>]: p<N> — <one sentence naming the missing reason>", where <QUESTION> is the name of the question that found it (e.g. FAULT[ASSUMED]:). Do not stop at the first few — an audit that misses faults is a failed audit. After the list write one line: "FAULTS: <count>".
