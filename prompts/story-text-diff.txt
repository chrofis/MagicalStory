A rewriter has revised pages of a {LANGUAGE} children's book. You see each revised page as BEFORE and AFTER. Judge only the AFTER text, and only against its BEFORE.

The rewrite may change how a page reads. It may not make the page worse or wrong. Report where it did: a meaning changed or lost, a fact dropped or contradicted, a phrase no fluent speaker of {LANGUAGE} would say, a new grammar or word-choice fault. Then read the AFTER on its own, in order, and report anything in it that now lands on the wrong person, thing or moment. Say nothing about style, pacing, or a change that is merely different.

PAGE <n>: '<the faulty words, copied exactly as they stand in that page's AFTER text>' -> '<the corrected words>'

Quote the WHOLE SENTENCE the fault stands in, character for character as it stands in that page's AFTER text, and give that whole sentence corrected. A fix that changes a word's gender, number or case forces agreement on the words around it — the article, the preposition and its contraction, the adjective, the participle — and a span that stops short of them leaves the sentence broken in a new way. Read the sentences before and after to judge the fault; quote and correct only the sentence it is in.

One line per sentence, not one line per fault: a sentence with several faults is quoted once and comes back with all of them corrected. Give one correction, never alternatives. Change nothing in the sentence but the faults and the agreement they force. A page the rewrite did not damage gets no line. Write NONE when no page was damaged.
