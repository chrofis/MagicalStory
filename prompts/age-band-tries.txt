[[premise]]# THREE TRIES (age 3)[[/premise]]

[[premise]]The child this book is for is three. Write for that child. These rules override any instruction
elsewhere to build several challenges or a complicated plot.[[/premise]]

[[premise:subject]]**One problem, met three times.** The book has a single concrete problem, plain enough to point at. It is a thing, a place or a creature that will not do what
is wanted, and it is different in every book. For a life-skill topic the problem is the outside event
that makes the skill necessary — something that happens in the world, never a feeling on its own, and
never the same request made three times. Three tries, no more. [[/premise:subject]][[mechanics]]Each try is a different kind of attempt, not
more of the last one — pulling harder after pulling is the same try twice. The first two do not
work.[[/mechanics]]

[[premise:agency]]**The child's own doing.** Before the third try the main character notices something about the
problem the first two missed. The third try comes from that noticing. Never luck, never a
grown-up stepping in to do it for them, never the problem simply going away.[[/premise:agency]]

[[premise:resolution]]**It resolves.** The third try works, and the problem is solved before the last page.[[/premise:resolution]]

[[premise]]**Small noticing, not cleverness.** What the main character notices is the size of a three-year-old's
noticing. Never a plan, a trick, or a child who reasons like a grown-up.[[/premise]]

[[premise]]**Kindness is the motive.** Helping an animal friend or another child is the natural reason to keep
going. Heroism at this age is being kind and not giving up.[[/premise]]

[[premise]]**A gentle obstacle, never a villain.** Nobody is unkind on purpose. What stands in the way is a
thing, the weather, or a size — not a person who means harm.[[/premise]]

[[premise]]**Feelings named plainly.** Say what the main character feels in the simplest word for it: sad, then
helped, then happy.[[/premise]][[craft]] One feeling per turn of the story, on the face and in what the body does.[[/craft]]

[[craft]]**Food.** Only ordinary food a child of this age eats, named plainly. Never anything a small child
cannot chew or swallow safely.[[/craft]]

[[craft]]**A life event is weather, not a task.** What changes around the main character, and what they still
do the same way. Never something they explain, put right, or are told the meaning of.[[/craft]]

[[craft]]**Ending.** The problem is solved and somebody is glad. Warm and small — no prize, no ceremony, no
line about what it means or what the main character has learned.[[/craft]]
