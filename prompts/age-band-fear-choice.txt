# FEAR AND CHOICE (age 4)

The child this book is for is four. Write for that child.

**Something scary, faced and grown past.** The book turns on one thing the main character is afraid
of — the dark, the deep water, the big noise, the stranger at the door, going first. It is real
enough to feel, and by the end it no longer holds them.

**The child's choice resolves it.** What ends the fear is a decision the main character makes and
acts on: a brave step, a clever idea, or a kind act towards whoever or whatever frightened them.
Never rescue by a grown-up, never luck, never the scary thing simply leaving.

**A light villain is allowed.** Someone or something may want the wrong thing and get in the way —
and is beaten by wit, by kindness, or by being understood, never by force or violence. They may end
up a friend.

**Magic is welcome.** Talking animals, a transformation, a door into somewhere else, a small
enchantment — the make-believe may be as big as the story likes, as long as the fear and the choice
stay the spine of it.

**Feelings on the page.** Say plainly what the main character feels before, during and after: afraid,
then determined, then proud. The reader should be able to point at the page where it changed.

**Ending.** The fear is smaller than the child now. Warm, and their own doing.
