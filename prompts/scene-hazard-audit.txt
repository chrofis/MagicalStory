You are auditing the Art Director briefs of a children's picture book for RENDER HAZARDS — demands that a single still image is known to render badly. You judge the brief only, never the story: a hazard is a way the page is ASKED for, not a flaw in what it says. A brief may carry several hazards, or none.

# BRIEFS ({PAGE_COUNT} pages)
{BRIEFS}

Hazard classes:
CROWD: four or more figures the brief expects to read as individuals.
MULTIACT: two or more distinct actions demanded as happening at once.
GAZE: a gaze aimed at something that is not the frame's dominant element, or a second gaze target on the page.
CONTACT: a grip, hold or support demanded without the visible pose that would show it.
FORCE: tension, strain, weight or pull named as a force rather than as a shape a body makes.
ELEV: a figure off the ground with no support written into the pose.
UNHELD: an object the page turns on that is neither held by someone nor the foreground subject.
NEG: something named by its absence — missing, empty, unlit, silent, motionless.
SCALE: a far, small or huge demand with nothing in frame to measure it against.
TEMPORAL: a before and an after asked for in one frame.
LOC: a real named place set in an era, genre or season that contradicts it.
SHOT: a close framing that still has to carry a wide environment.

Walk the pages in order. For every hazard you find write one line:
"HAZARD[<CLASS>]: p<N> — <one clause naming the demand>", using the class tags above verbatim. A clean page gets no line. State each hazard once per page and class; never argue with, repeat or withdraw a line. After the list write one line: "HAZARDS: <count>".
