# YOUR TASK

The arc stands above with its critique. A panel proposed solutions. Choose freely — one solution, a combination, or your own better judgment — and RE-TELL the story once: {ARC_LENGTH} numbered sentences, factual register, all the rules of the telling, none of the flagged faults surviving. Never edit or patch the old arc.

The size of the change matches the worst fault: a CRITICAL fault may rebuild the story; MAJOR faults are repaired inside the kept structure; when nothing above MINOR remains, the re-telling reuses the previous telling nearly unchanged, touching only the flagged sentences. A fix that costs suspense is a worse fault than the one it repairs — coherence is repaired inside the tension, never by draining it. The re-telling changes only what Fixing names.

Output, in this order:
- One line "Fixing:" — which faults, by what approach.
- One line "Keeping:" — the scenes and turns that must survive untouched; landmarks the arc placed inside the commission's world are kept unless Fixing names them; never add a landmark from outside that world or relocate the story to reach one.
- "Challenges taken:" then a numbered list, one line per challenge the arc builds on, a few words each.
- One line "Used:" naming which solutions you drew on, by panelist letter and solution number ("Panelist A Solution 2").
- "FINAL ARC:" then the numbered sentences.
- "CRITIQUE:" then a fresh blunt self-critique of the final arc, answering four questions as an eight-year-old listener: where does the story lose them — confusion, boredom, disbelief? Does each thing follow from what came before? Is there a question they need answered, with the outcome in doubt to the end? Are the figures people a child likes, roots for, and can tell apart? Report 3 to 6 numbered story-level faults that remain; they usually look like: an event without a cause, a stake that cannot be lost or that never bites (announced but never felt), a removable character, a rival who stops pressing, knowledge nobody could have, a premise the commission forbids, a character who is anyone — nothing they do comes from who they are, an interaction no real person would have — a reaction the plot needs but the person would not give. The last two are MAJOR by default; a main cast of interchangeable figures is CRITICAL. Numeric precision, sourced measurements and times, and the plausibility of a mechanism swappable without changing who does what and why are not arc faults — later stages fix those; never list one. Tag every fault [CRITICAL] — the story is broken; [MAJOR] — a real story fault repairable inside the existing structure; or [MINOR] — a blemish.
