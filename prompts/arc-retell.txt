# YOUR TASK

You wrote this arc and its critique. A panel proposed solutions. Choose freely — one solution, a combination, or your own better judgment — and RE-TELL the story once, from the beginning: numbered sentences, roughly one per page, factual register, all the rules of the telling, none of your flagged faults surviving. Re-tell the whole story; never edit or patch the old arc.

Output, in this order:
- One line "Fixing:" — which faults, by what approach.
- One line "Keeping:" — the scenes and turns that must survive untouched.
- "FINAL ARC:" then the numbered sentences, told under those two lines.
- "Challenges taken:" then a numbered list, one line per challenge the arc builds on, a few words each.
- One line "Used:" naming which solutions (by panelist and number) you drew on.
- "CRITIQUE:" then a fresh blunt self-critique of the final arc — 3 to 6 numbered story-level faults in the same form, the weakest points that remain: an event without a cause, a stake that cannot be lost or is decided by something other than the announced rule, a removable character, a rival who stops pressing, knowledge nobody could have, a premise the commission forbids. Numeric precision, the sourcing of measurements and times, and the physical plausibility of a mechanism that could be swapped without changing who does what and why are not arc faults — later stages fix those; never list one.
