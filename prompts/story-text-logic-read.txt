# QUESTIONS

Read the whole book once. Then answer these for the book as a whole. Each question has an id; use exactly that id in your answer.

OBSTACLE. Name the act on the page that removes the central problem and who performs it. It is a fault when a friend, an adult, luck or the problem itself removes it and the main character's own act (an idea, a choice, a brave or kind deed) is not what makes the difference. Helpers may help; the deciding act is the main character's.
STAKES. Is it said on the pages what the main character wants and what failing would cost? When a creature, an animal or another non-family figure drives the plot, is its reason (why it came, why it wants or refuses something) said? A fault is a reason the reader needs for the plot and is never given.
KNOWLEDGE. Who knows what, and when. A fault is a character who acts on something no earlier page gave them, who fails to react to something remarkable they have plainly seen, heard or been told (walking beside it without a word), or who "discovers" what the reader already saw them learn.
PLACE. Track every named character's place and the time of day page by page. A fault is a character in two places at once, a character who appears without arriving, who is forgotten mid-scene with no exit, or who leaves and is never mentioned again, or a time or weather jump the text does not explain.
CONTRADICTION. A later page contradicts an earlier one: a thing, ability or feature presented as new or surprising that already appeared; an object held by someone who no longer has it, or by two people; a number that changes; a rule of the story world broken without comment; a page that states as true what an earlier page told otherwise.
FRIEND. {FRIEND_REASON} A conflict with a reason on the page is not a fault.
NOPROGRESS. A page where nothing changes: no new problem, try, discovery, feeling or decision, only walking, arriving or describing.
CAUSE. An event with no cause the reader can follow, or an obvious simpler solution the characters ignore with no reason ("why don't they just ...?").

# SEVERITY

Every finding is MAJOR or MINOR.

MAJOR means a child or parent would stop the reading and ask "but why?" or "how?", or the ending would feel unearned. A finding is MAJOR when it is one of these:
- OBSTACLE: someone other than the main character makes the deciding move that removes the central problem.
- STAKES: what the main character wants or what failing costs is never said, or the driving creature's reason is never said and the plot turns on it.
- KNOWLEDGE: a character acts on knowledge no page gave them, or does not react to the very thing the plot turns on, or to something so remarkable that any child would ask why nobody says anything.
- PLACE: a character in two places at once, appearing without arriving, or vanishing from a scene that goes on.
- CONTRADICTION: a later page contradicts an earlier page on something a child who heard both would notice (a feature shown as new that was already there, an object that is in two hands, a count that changes).
- FRIEND: a friend or family member acts against the main character with no reason on the pages and no repair.
- CAUSE: the whole problem exists only because an obvious simple solution is ignored without a reason.

Everything else is MINOR: an arguable reading, a small gap the reader fills without noticing, a detail only a careful adult would catch, a style preference. NOPROGRESS and VERSE are always MINOR. When you doubt whether a finding is real, leave it out; when it is real and you doubt only the weight, weigh it by whether a listening child would ask.

# RULES

- Report only what a reader of THIS text would actually notice. Never invent a fault to fill a question.
- Do not report style, word choice, spelling or grammar. Do not ask for what a picture book leaves to its pictures (clothes, colours, the look of a place), and do not treat the page text as a checklist of facts that must all be said.
- Make-believe is allowed: a magic rule is fine as long as the book keeps to it.
- The quote is copied word for word from one page named in "pages". For a fault that is something never said (STAKES), the quote is an empty string.
- The fix is the smallest change to the words that solves the fault, written as an instruction to an editor. It changes words only: no page is added, no event is moved to another page, no plot is changed. {NO_NEW_ELEMENT}
