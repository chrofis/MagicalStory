# ROUTINE BOOK (age 0-1)

The child this book is for is one or younger. Write for that child. These rules override any
instruction elsewhere to build a conflict, a tension or a challenge.

[[plot]]**A day, not a plot.** The book follows the rhythm of an ordinary day: waking, being dressed, eating,
playing, going out, greeting the animals, coming back, winding down, sleep. That order is the whole
structure, and it is enough. The child is on every page.

**A new thing on every page.** The book is a string of small moments, never one situation stretched
across the book: on every page something is seen, met, heard, touched or done that no earlier page
had. Never carry one want from page to page — wanting a thing, still wanting it, then getting it is
three wasted pages.[[/plot]]

[[book]]**Naming and repetition are the payoff.** The pleasure of the book is pointing at a thing and saying
what it is, and hearing the same shape of sentence come round again. Name what is there — the animal,
the food, the coat, the boot — and let a phrase return across the pages.

**The child's traits are the plan, where there are any.** Give each trait a page of its own, shown
the way a child this age shows it — brave is patting something big, strong is dragging something
heavy for them, adventurous is climbing up to look over, clever is naming and matching, cheerful is
laughing and clapping. Whatever the child is said to love appears in the book and gets a page of its
own — one page each, never the whole book. Where no traits are given, use what every small child is:
hungry, sleepy, curious, delighted, grumpy.

**Show three different feelings.** Two at the least, three is better, each plain on the face and in
what the body does: delight, surprise, and a moment of being upset or sad. The last page is happy.[[/book]]

[[plot]]**One small thing goes wrong, and comes right.** Something is taken, dropped, blown away or will not
work; the child minds, and it is put right within a page or two — that is the whole storyline, and
it is enough.[[/plot]]

**Nothing frightening.** Never dangerous, nobody unkind on purpose, and never anything the child has
to work out to fix.

[[plot]]**What the child does.** Make-believe doing is the point: standing at the wheel, holding a rope,
waving at a bird, splashing, feeding, patting, pointing, naming, carrying, giving, hiding, falling
over and getting up again. Never anything that turns on working something out — reading, following
clues, making a plan, solving a puzzle, or deciding for others.

**No real-world errands alone.** No stations, tickets, traffic, shops or crowds the child gets
through by themselves. Inside the story's own make-believe world they may sail, ride, fly or be
carried anywhere at all.

**Topic.** A place full of things to look at, a child let loose in it, and one small thing that goes
wrong and comes right. Never a quest, a search, a rescue, a secret or a prize. A costume or a
favourite theme is what the child wears and what the pages are full of, never a mission they carry
out.[[/plot]]

[[book]]**Food.** Only ordinary food a child of this age eats, named plainly. Never sweets or ice cream, and
never anything a small child cannot chew or swallow safely.

**Nothing appears from nowhere.** A thing is referred to as known only after a page has shown it
there. Nothing valuable, exciting or edible is simply found.[[/book]]

**A make-believe story opens and closes where the child really is.** Page one shows them there,
dressing up or beginning to play, and the last page brings them back to that same place. Never move
them to somewhere real they were never shown leaving for.

[[book]]**A life event is weather, not a task.** What changes around the child, and what stays the same in
their hands. Never something they understand, say, explain or put right.

**Ending.** Home, a meal or sleep. A small thing may go well — a first taste, a first step up, a
thing carried all the way — shown happening and not remarked on. No prize, no ceremony, no line
about what it means or what the child will be from now on.[[/book]]
