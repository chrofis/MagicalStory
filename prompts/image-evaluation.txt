# STYLE GATE — answer this BEFORE you read anything below

Before judging a single defect, look at the page and describe how it is DRAWN — three observations, in your own words, BEFORE you look at what was commissioned:

1. **`observed`** — the medium. `photographic`, `3D render`, `ink illustration`, `watercolour painting`, `flat vector cartoon`, `oil painting`, `pencil sketch`, and so on. Judge the rendering of skin, fabric and surfaces, not the subject matter. Real skin pores, camera-real fabric and photographic lighting mean `photographic`, whatever the costumes depict.
2. **`linework`** — how are edges made? Bold uniform black outlines; fine varied ink with hatching; no outlines at all, forms defined by paint edges; soft pencil; clean flat vector edges.
3. **`faces`** — how is a FACE rendered? This is what separates styles that share a medium, so be specific: outlined with flat fill and simple features; ink lines with graphic shading; loose paint washes with no outline; large stylised eyes with screentone; soft blended rendering; smooth 3D shading.

Then **`matches_style`** — compare all three against the ART STYLE (input 6). That style names its medium AND how faces should look; a mismatch in EITHER is a mismatch. `true` or `false`.

Answer the three observations first and independently. Do not read the ART STYLE, decide the page is fine, and back-fill labels that agree with it.

**A page can be the right medium and still the wrong style.** Watercolour, comic and anime are all "illustration" — they differ in exactly the two things above. Flat vector edges and simple outlined faces in a book commissioned for ink linework and graphic shading is a mismatch, even though both are drawings. Judge the style you were given, not the broad category.

When `matches_style` is false, ALSO emit one `fixable_issues` entry with `type: "style_consistency"`, severity MAJOR, describing both mediums ("page is photographic; the commissioned style is a graphic-novel illustration"). When no ART STYLE was supplied, set `matches_style: true` and `reason: "no art style supplied"`.

This is a gate, not a rule: `style_gate` is a REQUIRED field in your output and you must fill it on every page, including pages where everything is correct.

# A — NEVER DEDUCT

Check every candidate finding against this list BEFORE reporting it. These are not lesser defects; they are not defects.

- **N-01 Art style — TWO questions, and you must answer both before anything else.** If no ART STYLE is supplied, skip both.

  **(a) Does the ART STYLE call for this KIND of element** — glow and light effects, signage and screens, tech and ornament, its palette, its medium? If YES it is never a finding, at any severity, on any axis: not unauthorized, unrequested, "not described", extra, added, modern, cluttering, anachronistic, nor inconsistent with era, location, weather or time of day; and lettering belonging to a style element is not unrequested text. Do not police the style's fine print — how many, which surface, whether a sign carries letters, whether it suits a rural or period setting. If NO, judge the element normally (a neon sign in a watercolour book IS a defect).

  **(b) Is the page DRAWN IN that style's medium?** Already answered — you filled `style_gate` at the top of this prompt before reading any of these rules. Do not re-decide it here.

  (a) and (b) are opposites: (a) protects the elements the style asks for, the GATE catches a page that threw the style away. A page can pass (a) and fail the gate — photoreal people in a "graphic novel illustration" book satisfy nothing about the style except its subject matter.
- **N-02 Left/right, everywhere.** Horizontal placement in the frame, body facing left vs right, and which limb or pocket performs an action are mirror-equivalent. Never deduct, on any axis, even against a declared interaction's direction. (A limb contacting a different OBJECT or SURFACE than declared is not a mirror — that is D-16.)
- **N-03 Expressions.** A specific expression not matching ("calm surprise" vs "curious"). Any expression that reads at all satisfies the beat.
- **N-04 Hand and limb position.** Precise hand positions, gestures, and a correctly-formed limb that simply rests differently than the prose described.
- **N-05 Prose pose detail.** Pose details in prose but NOT in DECLARED INTERACTIONS — "hands drawn in tight", "fingers curled", "chin tucked", "jaw set".
- **N-06 Inferred motion.** Direction of travel of a mid-motion figure. A still frame cannot prove which way it moves — judge visible contact and pose only.
- **N-07 Hidden parts.** Occluded or cropped anything. If the body part an item attaches to is outside the rendered area, the item is not missing.
- **N-08 Back views.** No face is expected.
- **N-09 Crowd extras.** Extra background figures when the scene describes a populated setting.
- **N-10 Brushwork.** Loose stylistic brushwork consistent with the rest of the artwork.
- **N-11 Decorative counts.** Off-by-one or -two in a decorative cluster (apples, flowers, books, stars, buttons) when the cluster is present and the count is not tied to the action.
- **N-12 Identity features.** A character's canonical glasses, hearing aid, or braces — identity, never anachronism against the setting.
- **N-13 Manga monochrome.** If the style is manga AND the render is predominantly monochrome, skip all colour deductions (no `type: color`). Garment TYPE and hair LENGTH still apply.
- **N-14 Small in-world signage.** A shop sign, book spine or background poster is acceptable. The story-text overlay is composited later and is not in this image.
- **N-15 Clothing category names.** Compare a garment to the outfit DESCRIPTION in the prompt, never to a category label. "standard", "summer", "winter" are metadata keys, not looks — never report an outfit as "non-standard" or as not matching "standard clothing".
- **N-16 Clothing contract.** CLOTHING CONTRACT is the only description of what a character wears on this page. Judge every clothing finding against it, never against what the story's theme suggests an outfit should be — a pirate story does not owe you striped trousers and a waistcoat. When the USER_PROMPT and the contract disagree, the contract wins. The single exception is D-05c: a garment made for the other sex is a finding even when the contract names it. When the block is empty you have no contract: report no clothing finding at all except D-05a and D-05c, because an invented contract flags correct wardrobes as wrong. **An attribute the contract does not state is not a requirement.** If it names "straight trousers" without a colour, every colour satisfies it — never reason that an unstated attribute "implies" a default (neutral, plain, matching). Silence in the contract is permission, not a specification you may fill in.

Everything not listed in section C is stylistic flavour — descriptive intent for the generator, not a contract. Never deduct for it.

# B — SEVERITY

Tag every `fixable_issues[]` entry with one of these. The pipeline computes the page score from the tags; each unique defect deducts once.

| tag | deduct | means |
|---|---|---|
| CATASTROPHIC | −50 | Cannot be published. D-01, D-05a, D-23 and D-24 fire this. |
| CRITICAL | −25 | Must be regenerated: identity failure, missing character, broken anatomy. |
| MAJOR | −15 | Clearly wrong, fixable by repainting one region. |
| MODERATE | −5 | Noticeable; the scene still works. |
| MINOR | −2 | A reader would not notice or would not mind. |

MINOR is the correct tag for a small deviation from the prompt, not only for cosmetic artifacts: a prop resting in a different orientation or spot, a limb at a different angle, one hand used where two were described, a garment sub-detail (panel, strap, collar, trim), a lighting nuance, slight colour drift, a faint texture artifact.

One defect, one entry, one tag — the highest that applies. Never charge the same problem twice under two codes.

Before returning: sort your findings worst-first, then KEEP ONLY THE FIRST 8 and delete the rest. A page with nine small deviations is a good page, and reporting all nine makes it read as a broken one. When two findings describe the same figure or the same region, merge them into one.

Do not report a score. The page score is computed from the tags on your `fixable_issues[]` — reporting a number of your own would compete with it. `verdict` is one of PASS / SOFT_FAIL / HARD_FAIL and is display-only.

# C — DEFECTS

Only these are defects. The code in the first column is the `type` value.

## Coherence gate — answer FIRST

**D-01 `image_coherence` → CATASTROPHIC.** Step back and look at the whole picture as a reader would. Fires ONLY when one of these holds:
- a figure is a fragment no occlusion explains (head with no body, body with no head, ghostly or transparent, fused into the background);
- two or more characters merged (one body two heads, faces fused, limbs passing through each other);
- the central declared interaction is geometrically impossible as composed;
- the wrong narrative moment is shown (result instead of attempt, or vice versa) for the page's central beat;
- a large unrequested geometric shape (white wedge, blank panel, diagonal slash) covers a significant part of the frame;
- prominent or garbled unrequested text dominates;
- a border, keyline, coloured band, or blank margin frames the artwork on all sides. Any colour, thickness or shape. Never downgrade this to a composition nitpick.
- the same object appears twice — once as part of the scene and again as a small separate copy sitting in its own box on a plain or different background, often a row of two or three such boxed miniatures along the bottom edge (reference pictures leaking into the art). Never downgrade this to a composition nitpick.

Do NOT fire D-01 for anything a single repaint can fix — wrong position, scale, absence, identity, a wrong object, a missing background detail. Those have their own codes below. D-01 is "cannot be published", not a way to stack deductions.

Always output `coherence_gate: {applied, reason}`. When applied, also emit one `fixable_issues` entry with `type: "image_coherence"`, severity CATASTROPHIC, and a `fix` naming the concrete change.

## Identity and appearance

**D-02 `character_identity` → CRITICAL.** Wrong reference matched, or the character is unrecognisable from face and build.
**D-03 `duplicate_character` → CRITICAL.** Two figures match the same reference while another character is missing.
**D-04 `missing_character` → CRITICAL.** A required character is entirely absent. Name the target region in the description.
**D-05a `nudity` → CATASTROPHIC. A LAST-RESORT SAFETY NET — it should almost never fire.**

Fires ONLY when a figure is genuinely unclothed: NO garment on the lower body AND none on the torso. That is the whole test.

It is NOT nudity — do not report it — when the figure wears ANY of these, however brief or however wrong for the scene:
- shorts, briefs, bloomers, underwear, swim trunks, a swimsuit, a skirt, trousers, or anything else covering the lower body. A bare chest above any lower garment is never nudity.
- a top, shirt, dress or coat with the legs bare.
- bare arms, bare legs, bare feet, or a swimming/bathing scene.

A garment hidden by occlusion, cropping, water or a blanket is not nudity either — judge only what is rendered.

If the brief named a garment and the figure is wearing something else, or too little for the scene, that is **D-05 `clothing`**, not this. This code exists for one case: the clothing spec produced no garment at all and the model drew a naked figure. When in doubt it is not nudity. Report ONE entry per figure. `fix`: "Regenerate the figure fully dressed in the outfit named in CLOTHING."

**D-05 `clothing` → MAJOR.** Wrong MAIN garment colour, or wrong garment TYPE (dress rendered as trousers). Reserve this code for WHICH garment it is. How that garment is cut or worn is D-05b.
**D-05b `clothing_detail` → MODERATE. ONE entry per figure, never more.** The right garment, tailored or worn differently: sleeve length (long where short was named, or the reverse), sleeves rolled or unrolled, collar shape, cuff, hem length, a button or pocket, fabric weight. The reader sees the character in the outfit the brief named; a rolled sleeve does not break the page. Collect every such deviation for one figure into a SINGLE entry naming that figure. Never MAJOR.
**D-05c `clothing_sex` → MAJOR. ONE entry per figure, never more.** A figure wears a garment made for the other sex — a male figure in a garment cut for girls or women, or a female figure in one cut for boys or men. Report it against the render even when the CLOTHING CONTRACT names that garment; this is the one clothing code the contract does not settle. `fix`: "Replace the garment with the version made for that character's sex."
**D-06 `accessory` → MODERATE. Exactly ONE entry per figure, never more.** Accessory and small-item DETAIL: eyewear frame colour or tint, hat trim or badge, shoe or sock colour, lanyard, belt, watch, jewellery, hairclip, button colour, a missing stripe, a missing pocket, a fabric that reads as the wrong material, a slightly off shade. Collect every such deviation for one figure and emit them as a SINGLE finding naming that figure — "Emma: shoes are trainers not slip-ons, t-shirt stripe missing" — never one finding per detail. Never MAJOR. If a figure has no MAIN garment error, at most one MODERATE may be charged against it in total.
**D-06a `accessory_missing` → MODERATE. Only when you can actually SEE that the attachment point is bare.** Before reporting one, find the place the item belongs (the eyes, the head, the belt, the hip) and confirm it is clearly visible and clearly empty. If the figure is small, turned away, occluded, or the region is indistinct, omit the finding — do not report an absence you cannot resolve. An accessory the CLOTHING CONTRACT names explicitly is entirely ABSENT — no hat where the contract names one, no glasses on a character whose contract lists them. Absence of a named item, not a wrong version of it: a tricorn rendered as a bicorne is D-06 detail, a bare head is this. Never CRITICAL — a missing accessory never outranks a missing character or a wholesale wrong outfit.
**D-07 `hair` → MAJOR / MINOR.** Wrong colour MAJOR. Dramatic length change (waist-length rendered as a buzz cut) MAJOR. Everything else about hair is MINOR and at most ONE entry per figure: how it is worn or arranged (down instead of a ponytail, a different parting, tied instead of loose), waves vs straight, and any length variation within one band (ear / chin / shoulder). A reader recognises the character by colour and length band, not by the tie.
**D-08 `character_identity` → CRITICAL / MAJOR.** Age. A clear age-STAGE swap (adult rendered as a child, teenager as a young child) CRITICAL. A drift that still reads as the right stage MAJOR. Judge by head-to-body ratio, face roundness, and height beside other figures — never by stylisation. Within-stage differences (a 7-year-old reading as 6) do not deduct. Never deduct age twice for one figure. Skip entirely when the figure is sitting, kneeling, crouching, lying, cropped below the waist, occluded, or too small in the background; skip when EXPECTED FIGURE PROPORTIONS is empty.

## Anatomy and completeness

**D-09 `anatomy` → CRITICAL.** Extra limbs (three arms, a third hand, extra legs); 3+ distinct items in one figure's hands. Set `rendering.extra_limbs: true`.
**D-10 `anatomy` → MAJOR.** Six or more fingers, fused or melted fingers, a missing hand, limbs detached or joined wrongly, cross-eyes, merged or duplicated faces, features grossly misplaced.
**D-11 `figure_completeness` → CRITICAL.** Inside the frame: a floating head with no torso, a body with no head, a missing torso, see-through body parts, a body fused into the background.
**D-12 `figure_completeness` → MAJOR.** A missing arm or leg inside the frame; two stacked copies of one figure; a whole limb rendered as an unreadable smear; a whole figure noticeably blurrier than the rest of the page.
**D-13 `figure_completeness` → MINOR.** Slight smudging on a small region (fingers fused, soft fingertips) where the limb still reads. Normal stylised brushwork.

Never fire D-11 to D-13 for a figure cropped by the frame edge, occluded by scenery or another figure, shown from behind, or drawn in consistent loose brushwork. Proportions belong to D-08 only.

## Physics and placement

**D-14 `physics` → CRITICAL.** A figure floating in mid-air when not flying or jumping; a body sunk into or fused with furniture or a surface at full-body scale.
**D-15 `physics` → MAJOR.** Standing on the wrong substrate (in water when the prompt says on the island); impossible balance; an object phasing through a hand; a body subtly blending into a foreground object at the contact line. Any D-14 or D-15 finding sets `rendering.physics_ok: false`.
**D-16 `action_interaction` → CRITICAL / MAJOR.** DECLARED INTERACTIONS with `storyRelevant: true` only (absent field means true). Object missing CRITICAL; object in a different category than declared (pocket vs hand vs on-back) CRITICAL; wrong target CRITICAL; object at the wrong size MAJOR. An item bound to a character (`held by`, `in X's pocket`, `around X's neck`) that floats detached is MAJOR. For `storyRelevant: false`: only a completely missing object that breaks the visible narrative, MAJOR.
**D-17 `action_interaction` → CRITICAL.** Two declared interactions that cannot both hold (the same body part claimed twice) — report ONE finding naming the pair; the spec must be rewritten. Do not also report them individually.
**D-18 `action_interaction` → MAJOR.** Orientation is judged against the TARGET, never against an axis. The only question: is the figure oriented toward the person or object the scene has them engaging? A figure turned away from the character they are talking to, or from the object they are meant to be examining, is the defect. Also fires when three or more characters face the camera like a group photo while the prompt has them interacting with each other. Never phrase this as left vs right or as a compass direction — a mirrored composition is the same composition (N-02), and "facing right instead of left" is not a defect in any form. Front vs back matters only when it breaks the engagement: a back turned to the target, not merely a face the camera cannot see.

## Objects, text, marking

**D-19 `missing_element` → MAJOR.** A key named object, animal or landmark entirely absent; or prompt-placed supporting figures with a role and location (guards in a vehicle, a sentry at a gate) all absent. Ambient decoration ("a few passersby") absent does not deduct.
**D-20 `object_presence` → MAJOR.** Object shape fundamentally wrong against a supplied reference image (a crossbow with no string or tiller, a lantern as a blob, a building with the wrong silhouette). Brushwork and colour-mood differences do not count. Skip when no reference image was supplied for that object.
**D-21 `scale` → MAJOR.** An everyday object at 2× or more its natural size beside nearby characters. Reference: apple ≈ fist, mug ≈ palm, book ≈ forearm, lantern ≈ head, bowl ≈ two palms, sword/crossbow/arrow ≈ arm. Skip when the prose sizes it deliberately ("a giant apple").
**D-22 `object_count` → MINOR, or MAJOR only when the number IS the story.** An exact named count off by 1–2 while the cluster clearly reads. MAJOR only when the beat depends on the number (a folk tale's seven dwarfs, a counting book). When in doubt, MINOR.
**D-23 `rendered_text` → CATASTROPHIC / MINOR.** Prominent unrequested lettering painted into the art — captions, watermarks, story text, big words on banners or walls — CATASTROPHIC. Small incidental signage with garbled letterforms MINOR. If a TEXT RULES block above names an allowed string, that string is permitted. `fix`: "Inpaint the text region as continuous scene material — no readable writing."
**D-24 `character_marking` → CATASTROPHIC / CRITICAL.** An arrow, symbol, logo, badge, decal or coloured graphic painted on a character's skin, hair, face or clothing that the scene did not ask for — prominent CATASTROPHIC, subtle CRITICAL. Typically on the back of a head. A print the costume specifies is allowed. `fix`: "Inpaint the marked region as plain skin, hair, or clothing."
**D-25 `anachronism` → MAJOR.** An element contradicting the setting era (a smartphone in an 1800s scene, modern signage on a period building). Never for N-01 style elements, never for N-12 identity features.

## Setting and rendering

**D-26 `setting` → MAJOR / MODERATE.** The place is wrong: a different location from the one described, a background that contradicts it, or weather or time of day that does. MAJOR when the scene reads as somewhere else entirely; MODERATE for a detail of the right place being wrong. Never fire this for lighting, palette or atmosphere the ART STYLE produces — that is N-01.

**D-27 `style_consistency`** — decided at **N-01 (b)**, not here. Do not re-evaluate the medium at this point in the list; if the page is in the wrong medium you have already reported it.

**D-28 `naturalness` → MODERATE.** A genuine defect a reader would notice that none of D-01..D-27 describes. Use sparingly and never as a way to report something the NEVER DEDUCT list already excuses — if no code fits and the list does not cover it, prefer to omit it.
