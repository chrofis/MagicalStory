# Life Challenge Story Guides
#
# This file contains specific guidance for each life challenge topic.
# Stories should help children understand and cope with these situations.
#
# Format: [challenge-id] followed by the guidance content.
# Edit freely - changes take effect on next story generation.

# =============================================================================
# BABY (Ages 0-2)
# =============================================================================
#
# Every section here is written in the register of prompts/age-band-*.txt — what
# the pages CONTAIN, not what the child should learn. None carries a line of
# morals: the routine band forbids a lesson and forbids a coda, and the rules of
# the telling forbid a stated moral at every age. The prose is background for the
# writer, never material to deliver to the child.

[bath-time]
**What the book is.** An ordinary bath, from the water running to the towel. The
pleasure is the things in it — the water, the foam, the flannel, the cup that
pours, the boat, the plug — each one named as it comes.

**What happens.** Warm water, being lowered in, splashing, pouring, being washed,
hair rinsed, lifted out, wrapped up. One page per step, and something new to look
at on each.

**The small thing that goes wrong.** Soap near the eyes, a toy floating out of
reach, water that startles when it first touches. Put right within a page — a
cloth, a hand, a reach.

**What it is not.** Not a child who refuses and is talked round. Not being clean,
and not why we wash.

**Ending.** Wrapped in a towel, warm, carried off. Nothing said about what it meant.

[first-foods]
**What the book is.** Learning to eat. The high chair, the bib, the spoon, the
bowl, and a first taste of a few ordinary things.

**What happens.** Being sat up, tied into the bib, the spoon coming close, the
first taste and the face it makes, the hand that wants the spoon, the mess. A
different food on each page, named plainly.

**Food.** Only what a child this age actually eats — puree, porridge, soft fruit,
bread, a cooked vegetable. Never sweets, never anything a baby cannot swallow
safely.

**The small thing that goes wrong.** The spoon goes on the floor, or a taste is
not liked and the face says so. Picked up, wiped, offered again. Never a refusal
that lasts pages.

**What it is not.** Not a fussy eater being won round, and not a case for
vegetables — those are different topics and a much older child.

**Ending.** Full, wiped, lifted down.

[first-steps]
**What the book is.** The days of learning to walk — holding on, letting go, and
falling over and getting up again.

**What happens.** Pulling up on a table leg, edging sideways along the sofa, one
hand held, the hand let go, two steps, down. Each page a new thing held on to or
let go of.

**Falling.** Falls happen, they do not hurt, and getting up is part of it. Never
frightening, and nobody laughing at it.

**What it is not.** Not one goal wanted across the whole book, and not a first
step saved up as a prize on the last page. Steps happen all the way through.

**Ending.** Carried, or the last bit walked to someone waiting. Shown, not
remarked on.

[first-words]
**What the book is.** Pointing at a thing and saying what it is. The naming is the
book; there is no plot underneath it.

**What happens.** Each page is one thing seen and one word for it — an animal, a
ball, a boot, the moon — said by the child, or said to them and said back. The
same shape of sentence comes round on every page.

**The small thing that goes wrong.** A word comes out sideways, or the wrong thing
gets named. Met with delight, never with correction.

**What it is not.** Not teaching, not a list of words to learn, and not a child who
could not speak and then can.

**Ending.** One last word, and then home, a meal or sleep.

[going-outside]
**What the book is.** Being taken out and brought back. The coat, the boots, the
pram, and a world of things to look at on the way.

**What happens.** Being dressed for outside, the door, the path, what is there —
a dog, a bird, a puddle, a bus, leaves, snow — and the way home. One new thing per
page, named.

**Outside is Swiss.** Rain, snow, wind, autumn leaves, a lake, a hill, a tram.
Ordinary here, never exotic.

**The small thing that goes wrong.** A mitten comes off and is gone for a page.
Found, or replaced by a warm hand.

**What it is not.** Not an errand the child gets through. No shops, tickets,
traffic or crowds they manage themselves.

**Ending.** Back through the same door, coat off, warm.


# =============================================================================
# TODDLER (Ages 2-4)
# =============================================================================

[potty-training]
**What the book is.** Learning to use the potty. The potty itself, the underwear,
the flush, the tap — the whole small apparatus of it, and a child who is getting
the hang of it.

**What happens.** The feeling that it is time, getting there, pulling clothes
down, sitting, waiting, the wipe, the flush, the hands washed. Each step on the
page, in that order.

**The small thing that goes wrong.** An accident — the potty reached too late,
wet clothes. Cleaned up plainly and without shame, and the next time it works.

**What it is not.** Not a child talked round to it, and not a race. Children come
to this at different times; nothing here is late.

**Ending.** Dry, in the big underwear, and the day carries on.

[washing-hands]
**What the book is.** Hands being washed, and the times of the day that call for
it: before eating, after the toilet, after outside, after the animals.

**What happens.** The tap, the water, the soap, the foam worked over palms,
between fingers, under the nails, the rinse, the towel. A song long enough to
scrub through belongs on the page.

**What is invisible.** Germs are too small to see and they come off with the
foam. Kept concrete — what is on the hands and what goes down the drain.

**The small thing that goes wrong.** Hands wiped on clothes instead, or soap
skipped in a hurry, and back to the sink.

**What it is not.** Not a lecture on illness, and not dirt treated as disgrace.
Hands get dirty from good days.

**Ending.** Clean hands, dried, and straight to the thing they were washed for.

[brushing-teeth]
**What the book is.** Teeth brushed morning and night. The brush, the pea of
paste, the mirror, the cup, the two minutes.

**What happens.** Small circles over every tooth, front and back, the tongue too,
the spit, the rinse, the brush put away. The song that measures the two minutes
runs under it.

**The dentist.** A person who counts the teeth and says they are strong. Ordinary,
quick, nothing to brace for.

**The small thing that goes wrong.** Paste squeezed out too thick, the brush
dropped, a back tooth missed and gone over again.

**What it is not.** Not a battle at the bathroom door, and nothing frightening
made of cavities.

**Ending.** Clean teeth, bed or breakfast.

[eating-vegetables]
**What the book is.** Vegetables at a meal — on the plate, in the pan, in the
soup — and a child getting as far as tasting them.

**What happens.** A vegetable is prepared and comes to the table, it is looked at,
a bite is taken, and the face says what it thought. Different vegetables across
the pages, different colours, named plainly.

**How vegetables appear.** Cooked, steamed, in a soup, in a stir-fry, part of a
meal, or with a dip. Carrots, cherry tomatoes and cucumber sticks may be raw;
nothing else is pulled from the ground and bitten into.

**The small thing that goes wrong.** A taste is not liked, and that stands — one
bite tried is the whole of what is asked. Tastes change with age and the book
knows it.

**What it is not.** Not a plate cleared under pressure, and not a contest at the
table.

**Ending.** The meal finishes, the table is left, the day carries on.

[going-to-bed]
**What the book is.** The last hour of a day, in order: bath, pyjamas, teeth,
story, goodnight.

**What happens.** Each step of the routine gets its page — the water, the buttons,
the brush, the book, the light turned low, the door left as it is always left.

**The room at night.** Safe and familiar. A night light is allowed. Whatever is
taken to bed — a soft animal, a blanket — is there and is company.

**The small thing that goes wrong.** Not sleepy yet, one more call from the bed,
the dark noticed. Answered once, calmly, and settled.

**What it is not.** Not monsters made real to be defeated, and not a child argued
into bed.

**Ending.** In bed, warm, the light low, sleep starting.

[saying-goodbye]
**What the book is.** Being left somewhere and being collected again. The whole
arc, both ends of it on the page.

**What happens.** The goodbye at the door — a hug, a wave, whatever the two of
them always do — then the hours in between with the person who is looking after
them, and then the coming back. The reunion is shown, not promised.

**What the child feels.** Sad at the door, and the sadness is not argued with. It
eases when something happens in the room, and it is gone by the time the door
opens again.

**What it is not.** Not a child who is fine straight away, and not bravery meaning
a dry face.

**Ending.** Collected, carried or walked out, home.

[no-pacifier]
**What the book is.** The last days of the dummy, and the days just after.

**What happens.** The dummy is what it has always been — the thing held at bed
and in the car. Then it is given away or put somewhere for good, in whatever
small ceremony the family makes of it, and the nights after are got through with
something else: a hug, a soft animal, a hand held.

**The small thing that goes wrong.** A night when it is wanted and not there. Hard
for a page, then met with the other comfort, and easier the night after.

**What it is not.** Not shame at having had one, and not the dummy made babyish to
be got rid of.

**Ending.** Asleep without it.

# =============================================================================
# PRESCHOOL (Ages 4-6)
# =============================================================================

[cleaning-up]
**What the book is.** The end of playing, when the room has to go back to how it
was. Every toy has a place it belongs, and the book knows where.

**What happens.** The scattered room, then the sorting — bricks to the box, books
to the shelf, the small pieces hunted out from under things. Made into a game or
sung through, taken in small pieces rather than all at once, and the room coming
back.

**The small thing that goes wrong.** More mess than expected, or a piece that will
not be found until the last moment.

**What it is not.** Not tidying as punishment, and not one child left with the
whole floor.

**Ending.** A room that can be walked across, and the next thing begun.

[sitting-still]
**What the book is.** The times a body has to keep still — story time, a meal,
sitting in a circle — and how long that is when you are small.

**What happens.** The stillness is asked for, the wiggles arrive anyway, and
something is done with them: a breath, hands folded, counting quietly, feet
pressed to the floor. Short stretches at first, longer later.

**What it costs.** Hard is shown as hard. The body wanting to move is on the page
— the legs, the hands, the shifting.

**The small thing that goes wrong.** The stillness breaks halfway, and is picked
up again without a scene.

**What it is not.** Not a child praised for being good, and not stillness held
perfectly on the first try.

**Ending.** The still part ends and the moving part starts — running, out, loose.

[sharing]
**What the book is.** Something one child has and another wants. What gets shared
is something the main character wanted to keep — a favourite toy, the last piece,
their own turn — and it is shared with another person, a friend, a sibling, a
playmate. Handing over something they never wanted is not sharing, and an animal
is not who it is shared with.

**What happens.** Both children want the one thing. It is asked for rather than
taken. It goes across, or the turns are split, and the playing that follows is
bigger than the playing before — two people, more ideas, more noise.

**What stays private.** Some things are not shared, and that holds. A child may
keep one thing back.

**The small thing that goes wrong.** The thing is taken instead of asked for, or
the sharing is regretted for a page before it turns.

**What it is not.** Not generosity announced, and not a child told what sharing
means.

**Ending.** The two of them playing with it, or with what it turned into.

[waiting-turn]
**What the book is.** A queue, a slide, a game with one of something and several
who want it. The waiting is the book.

**What happens.** The turn is wanted now and it is not now. The wait is filled —
watching the others, cheering whoever is on, counting, breathing — and then the
turn comes and is worth it.

**Pushing in.** What it does to the faces of the ones behind. Shown from where
they stand, not explained.

**The small thing that goes wrong.** The wait feels too long and nearly breaks.

**What it is not.** Not patience praised as a virtue, and not a turn skipped as
punishment.

**Ending.** On the slide, in the game, up at last.

[first-kindergarten]
**What the book is.** The first days somewhere new — the room, the pegs, the
teacher, the other children — a place that is strange on the first page and
familiar by the last.

**What happens.** Arriving, being shown where things go, and the shape of the day:
painting, singing, the yard, a story, lunch. The others are new too, and it shows
on their faces.

**The teacher.** The person who knows where everything is and who to go to.
Ordinary and available.

**Missing home.** Real, and it comes and goes across the day. Pickup happens and
is on the page.

**What it is not.** Not a child promised it will be fine and then finding it is.
The day itself does the work.

**Ending.** Out of the door, collected, the room already less strange.

[making-friends]
**What the book is.** Two children who did not know each other, and the hour in
which they do.

**What happens.** Standing near, saying hello, a name asked and given, and then
the thing they turn out both to like — a colour, a game, an animal, a way of
playing. The friendship gets bigger by being played in, not by being agreed to.

**When it does not take.** Someone would rather not, and that is allowed to stand.
Different friends for different things.

**The small thing that goes wrong.** An opening that lands badly, and a second try
that goes better.

**What it is not.** Not a lesson in how to approach people, and not a child who
had nobody and now has somebody.

**Ending.** Mid-game with somebody new.

[being-brave]
**What the book is.** One thing that frightens the main character, and the moment
they do it anyway. The fear is still there while they do it.

**What happens.** The thing is in front of them and it is too much. A breath, a
hand held, a smaller step first, and then the step itself. Someone else admits to
being afraid of something too.

**Asking for help.** Part of doing it, never the end of the attempt.

**The small thing that goes wrong.** A first approach that stops short, and a
second that does not.

**What it is not.** Not fear talked away, and not a child who turns out not to
have been frightened after all.

**Ending.** On the other side of the thing, still slightly shaky.

[new-sibling]
**What the book is.** A baby arriving, and the household it arrives into. What
changes, and what carries on the same.

**What happens.** The baby comes home. It cries, it is fed, it is held, it takes a
great deal of everyone's hands. The older child does what an older child can do —
fetching, holding a bottle, singing, showing the baby a toy — and gets time with a
parent that is theirs alone.

**Jealousy.** It arrives, it is said aloud, and it is not corrected.

**What it is not.** Not a child told that love multiplies, and not a baby who
sleeps through the book to keep it easy.

**Ending.** The two of them together, the house quiet for a moment.

[tattling-vs-telling]
**What the book is.** The line between fetching an adult and getting somebody into
trouble, drawn through the situations of an ordinary day.

**What happens.** Two kinds of thing happen. Someone is hurt, in danger or being
treated badly — an adult is fetched, and that is right every time. Someone takes a
crayon, or pushes in, or says something small and irritating — that one gets
handled between the children.

**The test.** Is somebody getting hurt or in danger. That question sorts them, and
when it cannot be sorted an adult is asked anyway.

**The small thing that goes wrong.** A small thing is reported and lands badly, or
a real one is nearly kept quiet.

**What it is not.** Not a child shamed as a tattletale, and not silence treated as
loyalty.

**Ending.** The real thing told, the small thing sorted out on the spot.

[understanding-rules]
**What the book is.** The rules of a household and what each one is for — safety at
the stove, health at the toothbrush, respect at the table — and a child finding out
why they exist.

**What happens.** A rule is met and it is asked about. The answer is the reason
behind it, given plainly. Sometimes the reason is a danger the child cannot see yet
and an adult can.

**Rules move.** As a child shows they can be trusted, a rule loosens and there is
more they are allowed to do. Different households run different rules.

**The small thing that goes wrong.** A rule is ignored and what it was guarding
against happens — small, real, and not a punishment handed down.

**What it is not.** Not a list of do-nots, and not a question treated as cheek.

**Ending.** The rule understood, or changed, and the day going on.

# EARLY SCHOOL (Ages 6-9)
# =============================================================================

[first-school]
**What the book is.** Moving up to the bigger school: a building with more rooms
than the last one, more people in it, and a timetable instead of a day.

**What happens.** Finding the classroom, the peg or the locker, the hall, the
yard. The shape of a school day — lessons, break, lunch, home time — learned by
going through it. Old friends kept and new ones met, which are not the same job.

**What it feels like.** Nervous at the start of the week and less so by the end of
it, and everyone else is nervous too whether they show it or not.

**The teacher.** On the child's side, and can be asked.

**What it is not.** Not school sold as an adventure, and not a first day that goes
perfectly.

**Ending.** The way out of the building known without asking.

[homework]
**What the book is.** Work brought home and got through: the table, the books, the
time of day it always happens.

**What happens.** The same place, the same hour, the distractions put out of
reach. A big task cut into pieces and taken one at a time. A break when it stops
going in, and back to it afterwards. Help asked for when something is stuck — from
a parent, a teacher, an older sibling.

**Practice.** Why the work exists is that the second time is easier than the first.
Shown in the child's own hands, not stated.

**The small thing that goes wrong.** A piece that will not come, and the
frustration of it on the page before the break that fixes it.

**What it is not.** Not homework as a virtue, and not a child who enjoys it.

**Ending.** The books shut, and the rest of the evening.

[reading-alone]
**What the book is.** A child reading by themselves. The sounding out, the
sticking, the going faster, and the book that turns out to be worth it.

**What happens.** Words worked out slowly, a page finished, then a chapter. A bit
of reading every day, ten minutes at a time. A library, a shelf, a book found on a
subject already loved.

**Pace.** Every reader began at the beginning and they arrive at different times.
No level of book is beneath anyone.

**The small thing that goes wrong.** A word that will not come apart, and comes
apart on the second go.

**What it is not.** Not reading praised as a superpower, and not a child who
suddenly can.

**Ending.** Inside a book, still reading.

[losing-game]
**What the book is.** A game played properly and lost. Losing is where the book
spends its time.

**What happens.** The wanting to win, the playing, the losing — and then the
minutes afterwards: the winner congratulated, the disappointment carried without
throwing anything, the going over of what to practise next. Someone good at the
game turns out to have lost plenty.

**Fair play.** Matters more than the result, and is visible in how the game is
played rather than said at the end.

**The small thing that goes wrong.** The disappointment nearly spills, and is got
hold of.

**What it is not.** Not a rematch won on the last page to make it all right, and
not losing treated as a lesson delivered.

**Ending.** Still playing, or arranging to play again.

[being-different]
**What the book is.** Someone who is not like the others in some plain way — how
they move, how they look, where their family is from, what they are into, what
they can and cannot do — and a group that has to make room.

**What happens.** The difference is noticed. It is asked about, or it is not, or it
is remarked on badly and that lands. Someone stands beside the one being treated
unfairly. The friendship that forms is the unlikely one.

**Everybody.** Each person in the book has something that is only theirs, the main
character included.

**The small thing that goes wrong.** An unkind word or an exclusion, met and
answered.

**What it is not.** Not difference celebrated in a speech, and not a child who
exists in the book to be accepted.

**Ending.** Together in the same game, the difference still there.

[dealing-bully]
**What the book is.** A child being bullied and the way out of it. Never the
child's fault, at any point in the book.

**What happens.** What bullying actually looks like — mean words, being shut out,
being pushed or hit, a rumour travelling. Then what is done: walking away, a
steady voice, friends standing together, and a trusted adult told. Telling an
adult here is getting help, and the book treats it as such.

**The bully.** Has something wrong in their own life. Explained enough to be a
person, never enough to excuse it.

**Safety.** Standing up happens in ways that do not put the child in danger.

**What it is not.** Not a bully beaten by force, and not a child who solves this
entirely alone.

**Ending.** Safe, with the people who stood with them.

[telling-truth]
**What the book is.** A lie told and then undone. The truth comes out because the
child says it.

**What happens.** Something goes wrong and there is a version of it that would be
easier. The lie is told, and then it needs another one to hold it up, and it grows
heavier through the middle of the book. It is owned up to. What follows is smaller
than what the lie was costing.

**Trust.** Comes back slowly rather than all at once, and that slowness is shown.

**Not knowing.** Saying so is allowed, and beats inventing an answer.

**What it is not.** Not honesty rewarded with praise, and not a punishment scene.

**Ending.** The thing said, the weight gone.

[trying-new-things]
**What the book is.** A first time at something — a food, a sport, a place, an
instrument, a group — and what the first time is actually like.

**What happens.** The not wanting to, the doing it anyway, and the clumsy first
attempt. Some of it is liked and some of it is not, and both outcomes are allowed
to stand. A new skill is bad before it is good.

**The small thing that goes wrong.** It goes badly at the start, and the second
attempt goes less badly.

**What it is not.** Not a new thing that turns into a passion by the last page,
and not trying framed as courage.

**Ending.** Mid-try, or on the way to the next one.

# FAMILY CHANGES (All ages)
# =============================================================================

[moving-house]
**What the book is.** Leaving one home and arriving at another. Both ends of it —
the boxes going out and the boxes coming in.

**What happens.** The old room emptied, the goodbyes at the door and at school,
the journey, the new room with nothing in it yet. Then the familiar things come
out of the boxes — the toys, the photographs, the bedding — and the room starts to
be theirs.

**What comes along.** The people from before are reachable: visits, calls,
letters, messages. The memories are not left in the old rooms.

**Sadness.** On the page, more than once, and not argued out of.

**What it is not.** Not a new place better than the old one, and not a child who
settles in a day.

**Ending.** In the new room at night, the familiar things in it.

[going-vacation]
**What the book is.** Going away and coming back. Packing, travelling, being
somewhere else, and the door of home at the end.

**What happens.** The bag packed, the journey with its waiting, the arrival, and
then the unfamiliar things — the food, the language, the beds, the rules of the
place. Photographs taken or a notebook kept.

**Missing home.** Happens on holiday too and is ordinary.

**Flexibility.** Somewhere else does things differently, and the family bends to
it rather than the other way.

**The small thing that goes wrong.** Something left behind, a plan rained off, a
journey longer than promised.

**What it is not.** Not a holiday without a dull hour, and not the trip summed up
at the end.

**Ending.** Through the front door, own bed.

[parents-splitting]
**What the book is.** Parents separating, and the months around it. The child did
not cause it — nothing in the book puts a foot near that.

**What happens.** One household becomes two. The moving between them, the bags,
the two bedrooms, the two sets of ordinary evenings. Both parents go on loving the
child and both go on looking after them, shown in what they do.

**Feelings.** Sad, angry, confused, and all of them at once. None of them is
corrected.

**Whose problem it is.** The adults', to sort out. The child does not carry it and
does not mend it.

**What it is not.** Not parents who get back together, and not a child who mediates
between them.

**Ending.** Settled in one of the two homes, an ordinary evening, different from
before and steady.

[visiting-doctor]
**What the book is.** A trip to the doctor, start to finish. The waiting room, the
call of the name, the room, the examination, and out again.

**What happens.** The steps in order — height and weight, the cold stethoscope, the
light in the ears, the mouth open — and sometimes an injection. The injection hurts
for a moment and is over in a moment.

**Being honest in the room.** Saying where it hurts and how much is what lets the
doctor help. Saying you are nervous is allowed and is said aloud.

**The people there.** Doctors and nurses are kind and they explain what they are
about to do.

**What it is not.** Not a visit with nothing uncomfortable in it, and not a bribe
for good behaviour.

**Ending.** Out of the surgery, the rest of the day.

[staying-hospital]
**What the book is.** A stay in hospital: the ward, the bed, the days and the
nights of it, and going home at the end.

**What happens.** Arriving, the bed and what is around it, the staff who come by
through the day and through the night, the machines and what they are for. Family
visit, and may stay over. Things from home are on the bed — a soft animal, a
blanket, photographs.

**The hours.** Frightening, sad and dull by turns, and dull is in there honestly.

**Healing.** Takes the time it takes. Home happens when the body is ready, not
when the child is brave enough.

**What it is not.** Not a hospital made cheerful, and not a child who is fine
throughout.

**Ending.** Out of the doors, home, slower than before.

[death-pet]
**What the book is.** A pet dying and the weeks afterwards. Death said plainly:
the body stopped working and cannot be mended.

**What happens.** The illness or the accident or the old age, the last day, and the
absence afterwards — the empty basket, the lead on its hook, the bowl. Crying, more
than once and on more than one page. Then the remembering: photographs, a drawing,
the funny things the animal used to do, told to somebody.

**Pain.** The animal is not in any now. Said once, plainly.

**Another pet.** Might come later, and does not replace the one before.

**What it is not.** Not a death turned into a departure or a journey, and not grief
finished by the last page.

**Ending.** The remembering, and an ordinary evening around it.

[grandparent-sick]
**What the book is.** A grandparent seriously ill, and the family around the
illness. The child is in the middle of it and is not told a story about it.

**What happens.** The news, the change in the house, the visits — or the cards and
drawings and messages when visits are not possible. Time with the grandparent that
is about being there rather than about the illness: a hand held, a story asked for,
the old photographs gone through.

**Who handles what.** The medical decisions belong to the adults. The child's part
is the love, and it reaches.

**Feelings.** Frightened and sad, said out loud to somebody who listens.

**What it is not.** Not a recovery promised, and not an illness explained away.

**Ending.** With the grandparent, or with the family, in an ordinary moment.

# PRE-TEEN (Ages 9-12)
# =============================================================================

[money-saving]
**What the book is.** Money set aside over weeks for something specific. The
waiting is the substance of it.

**What happens.** Money comes in through work and effort — a job done, a task
earned — and some of it goes into the jar, the tin or the account instead of being
spent. The goal is named early and priced, so the distance to it is countable.
Weeks pass. It is spent at the end on the thing it was for.

**Needs and wants.** Sorted in the child's own head as the weeks go on, on real
items in front of them.

**Money that grows.** Left alone in an account, it adds a little to itself. Kept to
the fact, not explained as a system.

**The setback.** A week when the jar is raided for something small, and the goal
moves further away by exactly that much.

**What it is not.** Not thrift as a virtue, and not a windfall that skips the
saving.

**Ending.** The thing bought with money that took months, held.

[spending-wisely]
**What the book is.** A decision about spending, taken slowly enough to see it.

**What happens.** Something is wanted in the moment. The three questions get asked
— is it needed, is it affordable, is it worth it — and the buying waits a day. The
same item is found at different prices in different places. Sometimes one good
thing is better than several cheap ones; sometimes the cheap one is plenty.

**Advertising.** Built to make the wanting happen. Seen for what it is doing, in
an ordinary advert on an ordinary screen.

**Regret.** Something bought in a hurry and not wanted a week later — the thing
sitting unused is on the page.

**What it is not.** Not spending treated as weakness, and not a child who never
wants anything.

**Ending.** A purchase made or declined on purpose, and the reasoning already
behind them.

[screen-time]
**What the book is.** Devices in an ordinary week, and what the rest of the week
gets. The screen is a tool and a pleasure, not a villain.

**What happens.** Too long on it, and what that does — worse sleep, sore eyes, a
shorter temper. Then the limits: times and places without screens, meals and
bedtime among them, and breaks that are actually taken. What fills the hours
instead — outside, a book, people in the room.

**Being present.** A conversation had with the device down, next to one had with
it up. The difference is visible rather than argued.

**The pull.** Real. Stopping is hard and is shown to be hard.

**What it is not.** Not a device thrown away, and not screens sold as harm.

**Ending.** Somewhere with people, the phone elsewhere.

[peer-pressure]
**What the book is.** A group pushing towards something, and one person deciding
for themselves inside it.

**What happens.** The pressure comes from people who matter to them, which is what
makes it work. It can push either way — towards revising together or towards
breaking something. The refusal is in ordinary words: no thanks, a parent blamed,
a different plan offered instead.

**Consequences.** Land on the one who acted, not on the ones who urged it. The
group goes home.

**Real friends.** Take the no and stay. The ones who do not are visible by the end.

**What it is not.** Not a crowd that turns out to be wrong about everything, and
not a child who is never tempted.

**Ending.** Apart from the group or back inside it, either way by their own
decision.

[body-changes]
**What the book is.** Puberty as an ordinary process: what changes, roughly when,
and the fact that it is on a different clock for everybody.

**What happens.** Height, skin, shape, voice, hair, sweat, moods. Each of them is a
sign the body is working as it should. Some of it arrives early and some late, and
being out of step with the year group is normal.

**Taking care of a changing body.** Washing, sleep, food, clean clothes —
practical, unembarrassed.

**Questions.** Normal, and asked of an adult who can be trusted with them. Nobody
is laughed at for not knowing.

**Moods.** Part of the same process. Being short with people and then sorry about
it is in here.

**What it is not.** Not a biology lesson, and not a body treated as a problem.

**Ending.** An ordinary day in a changing body.

[responsibility]
**What the book is.** Being trusted with something and the trust holding, across
enough time for it to mean anything.

**What happens.** Small things first — the homework, the chores, the animal fed —
done without being chased. Something is done properly with nobody watching. A
mistake is made and owned rather than handed to somebody else. The trust widens:
more allowed, further, later.

**What it feels like.** Being relied on, and the weight and the pleasure of that
together.

**The lapse.** One thing forgotten and the consequence following naturally.

**What it is not.** Not responsibility rewarded with a speech, and not a child who
never drops anything.

**Ending.** Trusted with more than at the start.

[managing-time]
**What the book is.** A week with too much in it, taken in hand.

**What happens.** Everything that has to fit — school, an activity, a job at home,
friends, rest — laid out where it can be seen, on a list or a timetable. What is
important and what is urgent are sorted, and the important thing goes first. A big
piece of work is broken into sittings.

**Putting things off.** The easy pleasant thing done first and the real thing at
eleven at night. Shown as it happens.

**Balance.** Rest and play are in the plan rather than what is left over.

**What it is not.** Not efficiency admired, and not a schedule that survives the
week intact.

**Ending.** The work done in time, and an evening with nothing owed.

[online-safety]
**What the book is.** Being online and the judgement it takes. Address, school,
telephone number and passwords stay private; that line does not move in the book.

**What happens.** Somebody friendly turns out not to be who the profile says.
Something is nearly posted that would be there for good — the test is whether it
could be read aloud to family. Privacy settings are found and changed. Something
makes the child uncomfortable and they log off, which is allowed at any moment.

**Cyberbullying.** Hurts the same as in a corridor and is handled the same way: a
trusted adult, told.

**The feeling.** When something feels wrong online it usually is, and that is
enough reason to stop and tell somebody.

**What it is not.** Not the internet as a threat, and not a child who solves it
without an adult.

**Ending.** The device put down, an adult in the loop.

[sibling-fighting]
**What the book is.** Brothers and sisters at close quarters. They argue; that is
ordinary, and the book is about handling it better rather than about stopping it.

**What happens.** The usual triggers — a shared thing, a room, a turn, something
that is not fair. Then the handling: words instead of hands, taking turns, meeting
halfway, and going somewhere else to cool down when nothing else works. One of
them stops long enough to see it from the other side.

**Different, not compared.** Each sibling is good at their own things. Parental
attention is not always even and it is not a measure of love.

**Allies.** They are also each other's — shown in a moment where they take the same
side against something outside.

**What it is not.** Not a truce that holds forever, and not one sibling who is
always in the wrong.

**Ending.** The two of them at the same thing, on the same side.

[being-active]
**What the book is.** A body that has been sitting indoors and a body that has been
moving, and the difference between how the two feel.

**What happens.** The dull heavy afternoon inside, then out: playing, exploring, a
bike, a swim, a hill, a walk. Not only organised sport — most of it is just moving.
Ten minutes counts and is where it starts. Someone is asked along and it gets
better for it.

**It gets easier.** The second week is less hard than the first, visibly.

**Balance.** Some sitting and some screens are fine. The body also needs the other
thing.

**Something done.** A hill climbed, a distance ridden, a stroke learned — the pride
of it shown in the body, not stated.

**What it is not.** Not exercise prescribed, and not a child turned into an
athlete.

**Ending.** Outside, out of breath, still going.

[getting-dressed]
**What the book is.** A child dressing themselves in the morning, all of it their
own doing.

**What happens.** Clothes chosen — and the choice is genuinely theirs, within
whatever the weather and the day allow — then the front worked out from the back,
arms into sleeves, legs into trousers, shoes onto the right feet. The awkward
fastenings last: buttons, zips, laces.

**How long it takes.** Long. Things go on backwards. That is what learning it looks
like and the book does not hurry past it.

**The small thing that goes wrong.** A jumper on inside out or two shoes on the
wrong feet, noticed and put right by the child.

**What it is not.** Not an adult finishing the job, and not being dressed by
somebody in a rush.

**Ending.** Dressed, out of the door, done alone.

[managing-emotions]
**What the book is.** A big feeling — anger, frustration, sadness — from the moment
it arrives to the moment it passes.

**What happens.** The feeling shows in the body first: tight fists, a hot face,
tears, the wanting to shout. It gets named out loud, which is where it starts to
loosen. Then something is done with it — breathing, counting, a pillow squeezed,
a quiet corner gone to. Afterwards, once it has passed, what happened is talked
through and the actual problem gets dealt with.

**Where the line is.** Hitting, kicking and breaking things stay out, however big
the feeling is. That holds throughout.

**Adults too.** Somebody grown up has a big feeling in this book and handles it in
front of the child.

**What it is not.** Not a feeling called bad, and not a child who is calm by
choosing to be.

**Ending.** Through it, sitting with somebody, the thing sorted out.

[whining]
**What the book is.** The same request in two voices — the whine and the ordinary
voice — and what each one gets.

**What happens.** The whine goes up, and it is hard to listen to and hard to make
out. The request is asked for again in a normal voice, and this time it is heard.
The plain words work better, and the book shows that rather than saying it.

**When the answer is still no.** It sometimes is, however well it was asked. The
disappointment that follows is real and allowed.

**The frustration underneath.** Genuine. It gets said in words instead — what is
wanted, and what is annoying about not having it.

**What it is not.** Not a child mocked for whining, and not a polite request that
always wins.

**Ending.** Said plainly, answered plainly, whatever the answer was.

[saying-sorry]
**What the book is.** An apology that is more than the word. Somebody was hurt and
the one who did it has to work out what to say.

**What happens.** The sorry that is only a word first, and it does not land. Then
the real one, which has parts: what I did, what it did to you, what I will do
instead. And then the making good — the broken thing mended or replaced, the one
who was left out brought in.

**The other face.** Before and after the apology. The difference between them is
the point of the scene.

**How hard it is.** Getting the words out is difficult, and that difficulty is on
the page.

**What it is not.** Not an apology ordered by an adult, and not the same thing done
again the following week.

**Ending.** The two of them back in each other's company.

[picky-eating]
**What the book is.** A narrow eater and a wider table. One bite is the whole of
what the book asks for.

**What happens.** Something unfamiliar arrives — a dish from somewhere else, a new
texture, a new smell. It is looked at, put off, and tried once. Sometimes it is
liked and sometimes not, and not liking it is a real answer.

**Tastes move.** What is unbearable this year is ordinary in two. Said once, and
then shown by somebody older at the same table.

**Cooking it.** A food helped with is easier to try. Hands in it, a pan, a knife
with somebody.

**The narrow list.** Eating the same three things is dull, and the dullness of it
is felt rather than pointed out.

**No pressure.** Trying counts. The plate does not have to be finished.

**What it is not.** Not a plate cleared to win, and not a child talked into a
liking.

**Ending.** One thing tried that was not on the list before.

[table-manners]
**What the book is.** A family meal and the small rules that hold it together.

**What happens.** Sitting down at the table and staying there, food off cutlery
rather than fingers, a mouth closed while chewing. Please and thank you for what is
passed. Waiting until everyone has theirs, or whatever the household's version of
that is. Asking to leave when finished.

**Why the table exists.** For eating and for talking to each other. The talking is
in the book — it is what the manners are protecting.

**The small thing that goes wrong.** Something spilled, a rule forgotten mid-story,
picked up again without a scene.

**What it is not.** Not manners drilled, and not a meal that is only rules.

**Ending.** The table cleared, everyone still talking.

[being-patient]
**What the book is.** Something that cannot be hurried, waited for from start to
finish: a seed growing, a cake baking, a birthday coming, a thing saved up for.

**What happens.** The wanting it now, which is ordinary and is not scolded. Then
the wait, filled with something else — another task, a count, a game. The thing
arrives in its own time and is better for the wait.

**Two kinds of now.** Help when somebody is hurt is needed now. A toy is wanted
now. The book keeps them apart.

**Interrupting.** What it takes from the person who was speaking.

**The small thing that goes wrong.** The waiting nearly breaks near the end, when
it is almost over.

**What it is not.** Not patience named as a strength, and not a wait shortened as a
reward.

**Ending.** The thing arriving, at its own hour.

[jealousy]
**What the book is.** Wanting what somebody else has, and the week that feeling
takes to settle.

**What happens.** Somebody has the thing, the skill, the attention. The feeling
arrives and is named out loud — what it is and who it is about — which is where it
stops growing. What they have does not subtract from what the jealous one has, and
the book shows both sets of things.

**Attention.** An adult giving time to somebody else is not love taken away. Shown
in an ordinary evening.

**Turning it.** Wanting to learn the thing, instead of resenting whoever can do it.
The wanting becomes asking to be taught.

**The other side.** Being glad for somebody, done on purpose, and how that feels.

**What it is not.** Not jealousy called shameful, and not gratitude prescribed.

**Ending.** Alongside the person who was envied, doing something.

[not-giving-up]
**What the book is.** Something hard, attempted more times than is comfortable.
Everything anyone can do — walking, reading, a bike, an instrument — was once at
this stage.

**What happens.** The first attempts fail. The failing is read as information: what
to change next time, not that it is hopeless. The challenge is cut into steps small
enough to make progress visible. Help is asked for at the point of being stuck, and
that is part of continuing rather than the end of it.

**The quitting point.** There is a page where stopping is the obvious thing, and it
often sits just before the thing works. The book goes through that page.

**Not yet.** The difference between cannot and cannot yet, in the child's own words.

**Effort.** What gets noticed is the trying and the ground gained, not only the
finish.

**What it is not.** Not persistence rewarded with a trophy, and not a skill that
arrives all at once.

**Ending.** It works, or the next attempt is already starting.

[being-left-out]
**What the book is.** Being outside a group that is having a good time. The hurt is
real and the book does not talk it down.

**What happens.** The group closes, on purpose or without noticing. Then what is
done: asking to join, finding other people to be with, and telling an adult when it
is deliberate and keeps happening. New friends or a different activity come out of
it.

**Not always personal.** Groups form and shift by themselves, and being outside one
is not evidence of anything being wrong with the one outside.

**How many.** A few good friends is enough. Everybody liking you is not the target.

**The other way round.** Later in the book, somebody else is on the outside, and the
one who knows how it feels does something about it.

**What it is not.** Not a group won over, and not exclusion excused.

**Ending.** With people who are glad they are there.

[taking-care-belongings]
**What the book is.** Things looked after and things not looked after, and the
difference a few weeks make.

**What happens.** School kit, toys, clothes, a bike. Put back where they belong, so
they are findable when they are needed. Something borrowed is treated better than
their own. The bag packed the night before, and the morning that follows from it.

**Losing things.** A favourite thing lost through carelessness, and it does not come
back. Going without, or the cost of replacing it, is the consequence the book shows.

**Trust.** Looking after things well means being lent and given more.

**What it is not.** Not tidiness as character, and not a lost thing returned on the
last page.

**Ending.** The thing found where it was put, at the moment it is needed.

[helping-at-home]
**What the book is.** A household run by everybody in it. The work of the house is
visible, and the child has a real share of it.

**What happens.** Jobs suited to their size — the table laid, the animal fed, the
room done, the washing into the basket. Sometimes done unasked, which is noticed.
The work is done together with music or a race in it, and it goes faster that way.

**Not a punishment.** Chores are what being part of the household looks like. Adults
do the ones they dislike too, in front of the child.

**The house is everyone's.** The rooms that are shared are kept up by everybody who
uses them.

**What it is not.** Not chores traded for money or for praise, and not a helper
thanked at length.

**Ending.** The house in order, everyone sitting down in it.

[dealing-disappointment]
**What the book is.** Something that did not happen. A plan changed, a promise
broken, a thing that did not come off.

**What happens.** The expectation, then the news. The sadness and the frustration of
it, which are allowed to sit there for a while. What happened cannot be changed, and
the choosing happens after that: an alternative found, a different plan made, the
day salvaged into something else. Sometimes what comes instead turns out to be good,
and sometimes it just passes.

**The outburst.** It is there, and it changes nothing about the situation. Talking
about it does more.

**What it is not.** Not a disappointment reversed on the last page, and not a child
who takes it well straight away.

**Ending.** Doing the other thing, the day going on.

[anxiety-worrying]
**What the book is.** A worry that is bigger than what it is about, and the week it
takes to get it down to size.

**What happens.** The worry works by making the thing seem larger and closer than it
is. It gets said out loud to somebody, and it is smaller said than thought. The
question gets asked of it: is this helping, or is it only making today worse.
Breathing, picturing it going well, talking to somebody — used on the page, not
listed.

**At night.** The worries that arrive in bed get written down or told to something,
and put aside until morning.

**Useful and useless.** Caution that stops somebody walking into a road is doing its
job. The worry about a thing that will not happen is not. The book keeps the two
apart.

**Too big.** A worry that will not go down is taken to an adult, and that is the
right move rather than a last resort.

**What it is not.** Not a worry argued away with facts, and not a child cured of
worrying.

**Ending.** Through the thing that was worried about, on the other side of it.

[caring-for-pet]
**What the book is.** An animal that depends on somebody, every day, for years. Not
a toy, and the book is honest about what that means.

**What happens.** The daily work: food, fresh water, the cage or the litter cleaned,
exercise, company. The dog walked in the rain and in the cold, on the evening when
nobody feels like it. Different animals need different things, and the needs get
looked up and learned.

**Reading the animal.** Happy, frightened, hungry, unwell — told from how it moves
and sounds rather than from what it says.

**The vet.** Part of the job, and it costs time and money.

**What comes back.** The animal's company, and it is shown rather than promised.

**What it is not.** Not a pet acquired in the last scene as a reward, and not an
animal that looks after itself between the good bits.

**Ending.** The animal fed and settled, and the child alongside it.

[comparing-others]
**What the book is.** Measuring yourself against other people, in the corridor and
on a screen, and what that measuring is actually made of.

**What happens.** What is posted is chosen, filtered and cut from the good hours —
the rest of the day is not in the picture. Comparison runs against other people's
finished results and the child's own unfinished middle, which is not a fair
measurement. Somebody who looks enviable turns out to have their own worries, and
the book shows that from close up.

**Their own progress.** Measured against themselves last month, which is a
comparison that can be won.

**Muting.** Unfollowing or muting whatever makes them feel worse is a sensible move,
taken in the book without ceremony.

**Strengths and struggles.** Everyone has both; only the strengths are on display.

**What it is not.** Not social media condemned, and not a child who stops caring
what anyone thinks.

**Ending.** At their own thing, not looking sideways.

[test-stress]
**What the book is.** The days around an exam: the preparing, the night before, the
room, and what comes after the mark.

**What happens.** Some nerves before it, which is ordinary and a sign of caring.
Too much of it, and the knowledge that is in there will not come out. The
preparation is done in short sittings over days rather than in one night. The night
before: things laid out, an early bed. In the room: breathing, reading the question
properly, leaving the hard one and coming back to it.

**The mark.** One test is one test. It measures a morning, not a person and not how
clever they are.

**A bad one.** Somebody in this book does badly at one, and life goes on around it.
What it shows is what to learn next.

**Adults.** Want their best, which is not the same as perfect, and say so.

**What it is not.** Not an exam aced through calm, and not marks treated as a
verdict.

**Ending.** Out of the room, the afternoon their own.
