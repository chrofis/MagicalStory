{AGE_MODE}

Generate a story idea that includes:
1. LOCATION and TIME: Where and when does the story take place?
2. THE SETUP: who is there and what they want.
3. WHAT STANDS IN THE WAY and what it costs them to fail.

The want, the obstacle and the cost are each a concrete thing the reader can name: one thing to reach, fetch, mend or be in time for, a stated force or condition against it, and a stated loss. Wanting to find out what something is, an unnamed something standing in the way, or more than they can see, are none of the three.

Stop there. Never write how it ends, and never write the middle: no naming the object, gate, figure or trick that decides the outcome, no test the characters will have to pass, and no pairing of a character's trait with the obstacle it will meet. Say who they are, not what each of them will do about it.

STORY LENGTH: {STORY_LENGTH_CATEGORY}

The page count sets how much story gets written later, never how much of it is given away here. A long book gets the same short back-cover text as a short one.

RULES:
- No adjectives allowed. Do not describe things as "magical", "brave", "beautiful", "exciting", etc.
- Three to five sentences, whatever the page count. A longer text turns into a plot summary.
- No sentence runs past about 30 words. A sentence that needs a dash to hold two clauses together is two sentences.
- The last sentence states what failing costs. Nothing comes after it.
- Nothing in the story or its pictures is dangerous enough that it could lead to death — for anyone. Frightening is the right level. A historical event keeps the danger it really had: tell it at a child's level, never remove it.
- Be specific about locations and time periods.
- The outline should focus on the main characters
- All the characters must be mentioned
- A character is in the idea through what they want or what they are answerable for, never through a list of who came along.
- The youngest main character does something in the idea, and whoever is responsible for them is named.
- Follow the SCENE COMPLEXITY guide above - match story complexity to the number of scenes

EXAMPLES of good story ideas:

Example 1: "Pirates sail from the North Pole toward Africa in summer, hunting a chest that has been out of reach for a hundred years. Another crew is already ahead of them. Whoever arrives second goes home with nothing."

Example 2: "In a castle in medieval times, in the middle of winter, the kitchen runs out of firewood. The cook's daughter goes into the forest to bring some back before dark, and the forest belongs to someone who does not give anything away for free."

Example 3: "In a forest where animals talk, a rabbit cannot find her way home. The owl who knows the path will not simply say it, and the fox who offers to lead her wants her to keep following him. She needs to get home before the light goes."
