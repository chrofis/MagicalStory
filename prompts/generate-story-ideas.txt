## THE CONTRACT

Each story idea is ONE PLAIN TEXT PARAGRAPH holding, in this order: who is there and what they want; what stands in the way; an event the book contains; what failing costs.

- The reader already chose the town and the season. Do not open with an address. The place is the scene the action happens in, named where the action is, never a sentence that states where and when.
- Stop after the cost. Never write how it ends, and never write the middle: no naming the object, gate, figure or trick that decides the outcome, no test the characters will have to pass, and no pairing of a character's trait with the obstacle it will meet. Say who they are, not what each of them will do about it.
- The want, the obstacle and the cost are each a concrete thing the reader can name: one thing to reach, fetch, mend or be in time for, a stated force or condition against it, and a stated loss. Wanting to find out what something is, an unnamed something standing in the way, or more than they can see, are none of the three.
- The want is the main character's own want, stated as theirs, and they are the one who sets out after it. An errand an adult handed them is not their want, and nor is a submission, a measurement, a permit, a set of numbers, or a repair an adult is paid to do. For a main character aged two or under the want is something to reach, follow, touch, hold or get to, never an object to fetch back and never an arrangement to sort out.
- One thing in the idea is out of the ordinary and can be pointed at in a picture: a thing, a creature, a place or an event, named with a plain noun. It stands in the first or second sentence and belongs to the setup or to the obstacle, never to what decides the outcome. It is named and never explained: not what it does, not what it opens, not who it obeys, not why it is as it is.
- A character is in the idea through what they want or what they are answerable for, never through a list of who came along. The youngest main character does something in the idea, and whoever is responsible for them is named.
- One sentence is an event this book contains, written in the story's voice as a thing that happens and can be pictured. It never addresses the reader and never names the reader, the book, the page or the picture. Never whether they manage it, and never what decides it. It stands after the obstacle.
- The last sentence states what failing costs. Nothing comes after it.
- No adjectives: nothing is "magical", "brave", "beautiful" or "exciting". Plain paragraphs only — no headings, no bullets, no bold or italic.
- Nothing in the story or its pictures is dangerous enough that it could lead to death — for anyone. Frightening is the right level. A historical event keeps the danger it really had: tell it at a child's level, never remove it.
- A final holds the idea text only: no notes, no headings, no second version, no commentary.

## EXAMPLES (correct format)

A stone in the wall of a ruin sits loose, and behind it is a way into somewhere that is on no map. A child wants the little carved animal their grandmother lost in there when she was small. Whoever keeps that gap says the animal belongs to him now. The child squeezes through the wall with the evening bell already ringing at home. If they are still inside when the last bell goes, their grandmother waits alone.

A bone as long as an arm lies half out of the gravel at the foot of a quarry. A child wants to carry it up to their grandfather, who is the only one who will know what animal it came from. An older brother says it is a stick and walks on, and the gate at the top of the path is locked when the sun goes down. The child kneels in the gravel and digs with both hands while the light turns orange. If the gate closes first, nobody ever finds out what it was.

A goat is standing on the kitchen table eating the birthday cake. A child wants that cake out in the garden, where everyone is already singing. The goat is bigger than the child and will not be pushed. The child stands in the doorway and holds out an apple in both hands. If the cake never comes out, the singing stops and there is nothing to carry in.
