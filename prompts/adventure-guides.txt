[pirate]
PROMISE: A pirate ship with its pirate crew is in the story, and the children sail on it or among them; a map, a chest or a costume alone does not keep this promise.
COSTUME: pirate (mandatory for adventure scenes)

Who lives here (pick one):
- A ship's parrot who repeats one sentence nobody aboard has ever said
- A ship's cat who has hidden something belonging to every member of the crew
- A castaway child on an island who has waited a very long time
- A young sea turtle caught in a net at the edge of the shallows
- A crab from the beach who has come aboard and will not be put back
- An old sailor with a delivery to make and no memory of who it is for [grown-up]
- A dolphin who swims ahead of the bow every single morning
- A monkey who takes whatever the crew needs most that day
- A stowaway smaller than the child, found among the barrels in the hold
- A gull with a hurt wing who cannot fly and will not be left

What turns (pick one):
- The tide turns, and what was easy to reach an hour ago is under water
- The wind changes and the ship must sail before the thing is finished
- The map turns out to show a place that two people both call theirs
- The one who was leading the way stops, and will not say why
- Something aboard is found to belong to someone who is not aboard
- The knocking inside the chest stops, and then starts again somewhere else
- The person who made the promise is the one who cannot keep it
- The island is smaller than it looked, and there is nowhere further to go
- The crew must choose between the treasure and the one who found it
- A door in the hold opens once with the roll of the ship, and will not open twice

Story entry points (pick one or combine):
- Full pirate adventure from start to finish
- Kids playing pirates in the backyard/park with makeshift props
- Dress-up party or costume day at school
- Dream sequence where kids become real pirates
- Imagination play that comes to life
- Time travel or magic portal to the golden age of piracy
- A pirate-themed playground or attraction
- Reading a pirate book that pulls them into the story
- Finding a treasure map that starts the adventure
- Museum exhibit that comes to life

Story guidance:
- Set on ships, tropical islands, or coastal towns
- Include treasure maps, sea voyages, friendly sea creatures (dolphins, parrots, crabs)
- Pirates as fun adventurers, not scary villains
- Focus on teamwork, exploration, and problem-solving
- Key props: treasure chest, rolled-up map, parrot, ship wheel, compass
- The idea is built on who is there, never on the props.
- The crew's vessel is a proper pirate ship: raised stern, bowsprit, tall mast, large sail with a pirate marking, deck big enough for the whole crew — never a rowboat, dinghy, or plain lake boat, even in a modern real-world setting
