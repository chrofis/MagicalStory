[pirate]
COSTUME: pirate (mandatory for adventure scenes)

Story entry points (pick one or combine):
- Full pirate adventure from start to finish
- Kids playing pirates in the backyard/park with makeshift props
- Dress-up party or costume day at school
- Dream sequence where kids become real pirates
- Imagination play that comes to life
- Time travel or magic portal to the golden age of piracy
- A pirate-themed playground or attraction
- Reading a pirate book that pulls them into the story
- Finding a treasure map that starts the adventure
- Museum exhibit that comes to life

Story guidance:
- Set on ships, tropical islands, or coastal towns
- Include treasure maps, sea voyages, friendly sea creatures (dolphins, parrots, crabs)
- Pirates as fun adventurers, not scary villains
- Focus on teamwork, exploration, and problem-solving
- Key props: treasure chest, rolled-up map, parrot, ship wheel, compass, telescope
- The crew's vessel is a proper pirate ship: raised stern, bowsprit, tall mast, large sail with a pirate marking, deck big enough for the whole crew — never a rowboat, dinghy, or plain lake boat, even in a modern real-world setting

[dinosaur]
COSTUME: not needed - use standard, summer or winter avatar

Story entry points (pick one or combine):
- Full prehistoric adventure from the start
- A natural history museum whose skeletons stand up
- A fossil found in the garden or on a beach
- Dream of walking among the giants
- A school dig or palaeontology club
- A time machine set far, far back
- A hatchling that follows them home
- A valley that time forgot

Story guidance:
- Set in fern forests, wide river plains, volcanic horizons, warm shallow seas, nesting grounds
- Use only well-known real dinosaurs, and only what a child already knows of them: Tyrannosaurus rex, Triceratops, Stegosaurus, Brachiosaurus, Velociraptor, Ankylosaurus, with Pteranodon in the air
- Invent no species, no names, no measurements - if a detail is not commonly known, leave it out
- Large meat-eaters may be present and impressive but are avoided or outwitted, never fought and never shown hunting
- Plant-eaters make the best companions: slow, huge, patient, surprisingly gentle
- Focus on curiosity, careful observation, respect for wild animals, being small in a big world
- Key props: fossil bone, footprint bigger than a child, nest of eggs, palaeontologist's brush, amber, ferns


[space]
COSTUME: astronaut (mandatory for adventure scenes)

Story entry points (pick one or combine):
- Full space mission from launch to landing
- A cardboard rocket in the living room that lifts off
- A planetarium show that keeps going after the lights come up
- Dream of floating above the Earth
- A telescope that shows something it should not
- A visit to a space centre on launch day
- A message picked up on a homemade radio
- A school science project that gets a real countdown

Story guidance:
- Set aboard a spacecraft, on a space station, on the surface of a moon or planet, in the black between stars
- Keep real places recognisable and simple: Earth from orbit, the Moon's grey dust and craters, red rocky Mars, ringed Saturn, the Sun
- Invent no facts about real places - for anything beyond the commonly known, invent an unnamed world instead
- Include weightlessness, tethers, airlocks, countdowns, slow careful movement in a suit, the silence outside
- Any aliens are curious and friendly and communicate without words - never invaders
- Focus on wonder, checking things twice, teamwork over long distances, looking back at home
- Key props: helmet with visor reflection, tether line, control panel, rover, flag, floating pencil

[christmas]

- Fetching and decorating the tree
- Baking, and the smell of the kitchen
- A journey home through snow
- Preparing a surprise for someone in the family
- A gift that goes missing or goes astray
- Helping a neighbour who would otherwise be alone
- The quiet of the evening itself

Story guidance:

- Keep families and traditions open - not every family celebrates the same way, and the story should not assume one

- Presents are never the reward for good behaviour - the warmth comes from people
- Key props: decorated tree, wrapped parcel, string of lights, baking tray, candle, snow boots at the door

- Focus on being brave about small dark things, seeing past appearances, kindness to someone who looks strange
- Costumes are a way of trying on being someone else, which is a story in itself
- Key props: carved lantern, sweet bucket, bedsheet ghost, torch under a chin, cobweb, pile of leaves, cat on a wall
