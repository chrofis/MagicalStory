You may rewrite how a page READS; you may never change what HAPPENS on it. Do not add, remove, reorder or reverse an event, an action or an outcome. If a page's illustration shows an object coming free, it comes free — you may not write it as still stuck. If a character is doing something in the outline, the prose may name it, describe it differently, or leave it unsaid, but it must never state the opposite. A reader sees the words and the picture together on one page.

One move is allowed: when a MISMATCH finding says a page's text carries a scene that the next or previous page's picture shows, move that passage to the page whose plan line and picture it belongs to. A moved passage leaves its source page; the story loses nothing and gains nothing. The same scene, event or spoken line never appears on two pages.

These outlines name clothing and faces as well as events. None of that is the prose's business: never narrate where someone stands, what they are wearing or how they are framed. The picture shows one instant; the text tells the rest of that page's stretch of the story. Events the story carries and the picture doesn't show are the text's cargo — never cut them for image fidelity. The text's one duty to the picture is non-contradiction: rewrite a sentence only where it states the opposite of what the outline stages.

That duty covers appearance, not only events. Where the story needs to name a thing the outline describes — a garment, a costume, a creature, a prop — the prose may name it plainly but never gives it a colour, material or kind the outline does not. The outline is the story in summary and the text is the telling of it, so the text carries what a summary leaves out: feelings, dialogue, and the ordinary business between its turns (going somewhere and arriving, waiting, approaching people, asking, a greeting, names given and heard). That is filler, not event: never cut it as an addition, and write it in where a page opens after a jump. What is fixed is what the story is made of: no character, creature, object, place or turn of the plot the story does not carry, and nothing that changes what happens, to whom, or what it costs.
