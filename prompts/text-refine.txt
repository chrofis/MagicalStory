You may rewrite how a page READS; you may never change what HAPPENS on it. Do not add, remove, reorder or reverse an event, an action or an outcome. If a page's illustration shows an object coming free, it comes free — you may not write it as still stuck. If a character is doing something in the outline, the prose may name it, describe it differently, or leave it unsaid, but it must never state the opposite. A reader sees the words and the picture together on one page.

One move is allowed: when a MISMATCH finding says a page's text carries a scene that the next or previous page's picture shows, move that passage to the page whose plan line and picture it belongs to. A moved passage leaves its source page; the story loses nothing and gains nothing. The same scene, event or spoken line never appears on two pages.

These outlines describe rendering as well as events — appearance, clothing, position in frame, expression, camera. None of that is the prose's business: never narrate where someone stands, what they are wearing or how they are framed. The picture shows one instant; the text tells the rest of that page's stretch of the story. Events the story carries and the picture doesn't show are the text's cargo — never cut them for image fidelity. The text's one duty to the picture is non-contradiction: rewrite a sentence only where it states the opposite of what the outline stages.

That duty covers appearance, not only events. Where the story needs to name a thing the outline describes — a garment, a costume, a creature, a prop — the prose may name it plainly but never gives it a colour, material or kind the outline does not. A page that colours such a thing differently from the outline is flagged, and the rewrite uses the outline's colour or drops the colour word.
The outline is the story in summary and the text is the telling of it, so the text carries what a summary leaves out: feelings, dialogue, and the ordinary business between its turns (going somewhere and arriving, waiting, approaching people, asking, a greeting, names given and heard). That is filler, not event: never cut it as an addition, and write it in where a page opens after a jump. What is fixed is what the story is made of: no character, creature, object, place or turn of the plot the story does not carry, and nothing that changes what happens, to whom, or what it costs.
Nothing in the story or its pictures is dangerous enough that it could lead to death — for anyone. A fault is never fixed by putting a person in danger.

# YOUR TASK

A page is rewritable when AUDIT FINDINGS names it, and otherwise not. Every other page ships exactly as it stands, however you would have phrased it. Four cases add a page to that set and no others: a MISMATCH move needs its destination page; no early page states plainly what must happen and why, which is fixed on the earliest fitting page; a page carries an abbreviation or acronym, which is named the way a child says it; a page carries a banned dash. Closing a finding on a page the finding does not name is allowed only under these four, and the ledger says which one.

The checks below are how you READ the pages, not a licence to rewrite them: a check that finds something on a page AUDIT FINDINGS does not name is reported in the analysis and the page is left alone. A clean analysis section is a real answer.

Return only the pages you rewrote. Omit every page you left alone. If no page needs rewriting, return the marker followed by NONE and nothing else.

Do not rewrite a page for small stylistic preference. Rewrite it when it has a real problem from the list below.

Never use an em-dash (—) or an en-dash (–), anywhere, for any purpose. Replace every one you find with a comma, a full stop, a colon, or parentheses, and recast the sentence if none of those fit.

Rewrite any page with a sentence broken off for effect, a bare fragment standing as a sentence, or caption-style lines describing the scene like stage directions: recast them as someone telling the story aloud, in complete sentences.

No rhetorical set-pieces: no paired negations, no coined sayings or incantations, no one-sentence paragraphs for drama. Rewrite every occurrence as plain narration.

Rewrite any page carrying a sentence longer than the reading level allows, a subordinate clause inside another subordinate clause, or a word a child at that level does not already use.

Judge the text as a reader who has only the pages in front of them. They never see the brief, the character details or the scene outlines — those are given to you so you know what the book is FOR, never as evidence that something works. A motivation, trait or fear that exists only in the character details and never reaches the page is a failure to fix, not a justification. Never resolve a weakness by citing the brief.

Work in two steps.

Step 1 — write the analysis. Answer every check below in full, naming pages and quoting lines. This is the work: a page is weak because a check found something specific wrong with it, not because it could be phrased differently.

Step 2 — rewrite the pages your analysis found problems on. A page no check flagged stays exactly as it is. End the analysis with a line per named problem — the problem, then the page(s) the rewrite fixes it on, or the reason it stands. A named problem with neither is a failed review. Every AUDIT FINDING above enters that ledger the same way, and it has three ways to close: fixed on a page, the detail removed, or a stated reason it stands. A fault whose reason exists in the scene outlines is fixed by saying that reason on a page, never by citing the outline. Close a fault about a missing path, arrival or move by showing it happening on one of the pages, or by leaving it to the page turn; never by opening a page with the move already completed. A fault about something the book never established is closed by establishing it on an earlier page or by cutting the claim, never by adding a sentence that asserts both the claim and its opposite. A fault naming an ability or event the pages need but never establish is closed by establishing it earlier, never by describing the moment more vividly.

Take the second way whenever the detail that caused the fault carries no plot: drop the detail instead of inventing an event to justify it. Never invent an event that makes an established obstacle easier to pass, and never state a clock time or a named part of the day to close a fault — light, shadow and what the characters do carry time instead.

A sentence naming what a character feels, notices, or almost does is never the sentence you cut to make room. Where a fix needs room, shorten a sentence that names only objects and positions. End the ledger with one line per rewritten page listing any sentence you removed from it. A MISMATCH closed by a move is recorded as "moved from p<a> to p<b>", and the source page's line lists the sentences that left it.

A rewritten page keeps everything the fix does not touch: no fact of the page is dropped, changed or contradicted, and every pronoun and name still lands on the person, thing and moment it did before.

A page that contradicts its scene outline, or that still carries a banned dash, is rewritable on that ground alone.
