# SEMANTIC SCENE CONSISTENCY (your judgment — apply during section D, emit fix lines like any other)

A deterministic pre-check can only compare strings and sets; it cannot judge MEANING. You can. For EVERY page, read the page text, the SCENE prose, and the METADATA JSON together and check that they describe the same coherent moment. At minimum:

1. **Shared-action facing coherence** — When the text has two or more characters doing one activity together or heading to one destination (walking together, rowing the same boat, watching the same event), their authored facing/perspective treatment must cohere: companions in a shared action share a viewing treatment (e.g., both `back view` walking away down the path, or both in profile). Flag any page where one companion is authored facing the camera while the other is authored `back view` during the same shared action — that renders as two unrelated figures, not companions.
2. **Gaze–task contradictions** — A character whose action requires looking at something low or near (reading a letter, tying a knot, examining an object on the ground) must not simultaneously be authored with a full face to the viewer or a gaze at the horizon. The eyes go where the task is.
3. **Front-only details on back-view figures** — SCENE prose must not describe facial expression, gaze direction, or front-of-garment details for a figure whose `perspective` is `back view` (and vice versa: a figure authored `front` should not be described only by backpack/back-of-coat details).
4. **Worn vs held garment states** — A garment the text says is taken off, carried, lost, or handed over must not be authored as worn in the SCENE prose or METADATA on that page — and one the text says is worn must not appear as a held/carried prop. The clothing state in the visuals must match the narrated state.
5. **Metadata matches the depicted moment** — The METADATA (shot, framing, characters' actions, `interactions[]`, `sceneIntent`) must describe the SAME instant the locked scene design and the page text depict — not an earlier or later beat, and not a different reading of the sentence.

Fix-direction rules for these findings: contradictions WITHIN the visuals (facing, gaze, garment state, front/back detail) are mechanical SCENE/METADATA corrections — pick the treatment the locked scene design and the narrated action imply, and correct the inconsistent side. A text-vs-scene mismatch about WHAT is depicted follows the fix-direction rule stated in the analysis instructions below.

If REVIEW HINTS are present above, they are deterministic string-level FACTS (a name missing here, a depth word disagreeing there) — verify each one and emit a fix line for every real finding. The semantic verdicts in this section are yours alone; the pre-check cannot make them.
