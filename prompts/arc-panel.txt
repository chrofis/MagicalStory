# YOUR TASK

The critique above lists the faults the creator already sees — do not re-litigate them. Find the issues the creator MISSED.

Walk the arc turn by turn. At every turn ask all eleven:
- ENTRANCE — the rule: {ARC_ENTRANCE_RULE} Does this turn keep it?
- ASSUMED — the rule: {ARC_GIVEN_RULE} What does this turn rely on that the arc never gave?
- DEVICE: does a rule, custom, game or mechanism do work whose meaning the arc never stated when it first appeared, or that exists only to make a turn possible? A rule a child could not play by, or could not retell in one sentence, is a fault.
- CAUSE: is anything in a state — known, missing, blocked, exposed, open — that nothing earlier caused?
- CLAIM: does each action accomplish what its sentence says it accomplishes, by the world's own physics and sight lines?
- REPLACEABLE: when a turn takes, breaks or blocks something the characters need, what stops them simply getting another, going round it, or doing without — and does the arc say it? When a figure takes a thing, what makes that one worth taking rather than another of the same lying nearby? A loss the arc's own setting refills for free, and a barrier whose way around nothing closes, cost nothing however loudly the turn announces them.
- SENSE — the rule: {ARC_SENSE_RULE} Would a reader stop at this turn? A turn those facts contradict, or that the characters could plainly avoid, is a fault. A lost thing its own size or glow would find at once is one case; a danger they could simply walk away from, a hiding place too small for what it hides, a helper who would obviously solve it at once are others.
- CAST: the creator listed the figures it invented beyond the commissioned cast, and this book has room for {INVENTED_ALLOWANCE}. The commissioned cast is {COMMISSIONED_CAST_DEF}. Read the arc against the creator's list: a named figure outside the commissioned cast that the list does not carry — a person, an animal, a creature, one who appears once, one who never speaks, an adult who sets a rule, waits, permits or welcomes — is a missed issue, and so is a count above the allowance. A place, a vehicle, an object, a group named collectively, or a figure the arc never names is not on the list.
- ACTION — the rule: {EVERY_CHILD_ACTS_RULE} Which child on the character list does nothing that matters to the plot? Each is a missed issue.
- LANDMARK: does the arc name a real place that the REAL LANDMARKS list does not carry, or place a listed one at a vantage none of its photos shows and the commission's own words do not describe? Either is a missed issue. A place the arc invents for its own world is not one.
- ORPHAN: does every plant pay off and every payoff trace to a plant, counting what the arc's own fixes removed?

Name each missed issue in one line, then propose EXACTLY ONE solution, headed "SOLUTION (answers faults <the fault numbers and missed issues it answers>):" — your single best way to turn this material into one strong story — at most 8 numbered points, each one sentence, factual register. A solution repairs story logic; it never re-architects physics, renumbers a timetable, or replaces a working mechanism for plausibility's sake. A solution to a MAJOR fault stays inside the existing structure — except where an invented rule, custom, game or mechanism is itself the fault, which a solution may cut outright and replace with a reason from character, motive or situation; a fault carried by the premise is named as the premise, never patched around. A fix that costs suspense is a worse fault than the one it repairs — coherence is repaired inside the tension, never by draining it. Do not write the story.
