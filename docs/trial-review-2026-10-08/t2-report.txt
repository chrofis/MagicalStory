===== STORY TEXT =====
--- Page 1 ---
Ethan ran up the steep path towards Ruine Stein, kicking through the yellow leaves. Above him the old tower stood against the sky with its flag, and he had one plan. He would be first to touch the top wall. Round the last bend a goose stood in the middle of the path. Its back was grey and its chest was white, and its orange beak was closed. Its round dark eyes looked at him from under a smooth, level brow. "Excuse me," said Ethan, puffing. "I am going up." The goose did not move. Ethan took a big breath. "Shoo! I am running to the top!" The goose opened its beak and hissed, and its wings lifted a little from its sides. Ethan stopped with his feet in the leaves.

--- Page 2 ---
"Shoo! Shoo! Let me past!" Ethan shouted louder this time, and the goose hissed louder back. He stepped to the left, and the goose stepped to the left. He stepped to the right, and the goose stepped to the right as well. Ethan stamped his foot in the leaves and waved both arms. The goose spread its wings wide and stretched out its neck, and its beak opened in another long hiss. Ethan backed away. His legs had been ready to run all the way up, but there was nowhere to run. The leaves lay still on the path between them, and the tower stayed far above.

--- Page 3 ---
On the bottom step of the low wall, Ethan sat down hard. The goose stood where it was, as if it meant to stay all afternoon. A few leaves drifted down and landed on the path. "I will never get up there," said Ethan. He rested his chin on his knees and looked at the tower, which was a long way off and not getting any closer. His feet still wanted to run. Beside him the grey wall ran up along the path, made of stones that were round and square and lumpy. He looked at them one by one, and his chin stayed on his knees.

--- Page 4 ---
"One," said Ethan, and he touched the first stone in the wall. It was cold and rough under his finger. "Two." He moved along to the next. The goose turned its head and looked at him. Ethan kept going, touching each stone and saying its number out loud, slowly, so that none was missed. "Three. Four. Five." The goose lowered its wings and folded them against its sides. A leaf landed on its back, and it did not shake it off. "Six," said Ethan. "Seven." The path stayed quiet. Ethan had not shouted for a long while, and he had not noticed.

--- Page 5 ---
At the ninth stone the goose bent its neck to peck at a leaf, and the path beside it opened up. Ethan's legs jumped and his body leaned forward. He was nearly up on his toes and away. Then he stopped himself and laid his hand flat on the tenth stone, the last one in the wall. "Ten," he said. The goose lifted its head and looked at him. It shook its tail, closed its beak and strolled off the path into the leaves under the beech tree. It began to peck at the ground there, and it did not look back.

--- Page 6 ---
Ethan ran. The path was clear and the leaves flew up round his feet, and soon he was at the top. He laid both hands on the tower wall, which was cool and rough like the stones below. Over the wall lay the roofs of Baden, the church towers and the river, all gold in the afternoon light. The flag moved above him. Behind him the goose came strolling up the path, pecking at the leaves as it came. "I touched it first," said Ethan. "I counted ten stones to get here. I will count the steps going down, goose. You can come too."
