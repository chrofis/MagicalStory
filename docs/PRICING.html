What one page really costs (Grok 2, current). A page is normally two provider renders, not one: the empty-scene background at $0.02 plus the page image at $0.04. Assume $0.06 per finished page before evals, repairs and text. A single user-triggered image operation (regenerate, prompt-edit, cover edit, character repair, title paint) is one call: $0.02 for an edit, $0.04 for a full render.

Margin on the 2-credit operations is thinner than it looks. 2 credits is CHF 0.050 – 0.067 of package value against ~$0.04 (≈ CHF 0.032 at ~0.80 CHF/USD) for a render — roughly 1.6–2.1×, and closer to break-even on the composite cover path that fires two calls. The old "3–4× margin" note in server/config/credits.js dated from the $0.018 Grok 1.x era and has been corrected.

⚠ The itemised table above is a 2026-05 calibration and is stale. Measured cost of a full story today is ~USD 4.2 – 4.9, i.e. roughly the credit price — margin on story generation is close to zero and printed books are the revenue line. Per-image operations (regenerate / repair / title paint) are the profitable ones: 2 credits = CHF 0.05 – 0.067 against ~CHF 0.018 for a single Grok call.

6. Referral economics

Configured in server/config/credits.js → REFERRAL:

  • Buyer gets CHF 10 off the printed book at checkout.
  • Referrer gets 350 credits (= CHF 10 starter pack) granted on confirmed payment.
  • One referral code per buyer, ever (users.referred_by). No self-referral. Code format: MagicName{NNN} with 900 slots per first-name.

Endpoints in server/routes/print.js: GET /api/referral/my-code, POST /api/referral/validate. Webhook in server.js credits the referrer transactionally with an atomic referred_by lock.
