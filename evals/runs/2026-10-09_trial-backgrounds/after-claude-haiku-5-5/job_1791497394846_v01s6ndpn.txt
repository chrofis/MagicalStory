--- Page 1 ---
TEXT:
Mia clattered down the lane in his tall black boots, a parrot tucked under one arm. He had been saving the parrot for a special job, and today was the day. On the low wall outside the church sat a boy on his own. He was not kicking his heels or throwing stones like the children who had walked home from school. He only watched the bus pull away from the stop. Mia marched up to the wall and raised the parrot high. "Ahoy!" he called. "I am Captain Mia, and this is my parrot. My ship needs a crew, and you look like a sailor."

Beside the white church, a cold breeze rustled the fallen leaves across the grass. The boy slid off the wall and looked at the parrot without taking it. "I'm Jonas," he said. "I'm not allowed to climb anything." Mia nodded. "Then you can be my first mate," he said. "First mates stay on the ground and hold the rope." Jonas stepped closer and stretched out his arms. Mia held the parrot out. A strong gust swept over the grass, lifted the parrot from his hand, and carried it up and over the roof of Kirche Rohrdorf. Mia ran to the wall and stared at the far side of the roof.

Behind the church hall, a pile of wooden crates waited by the fence. Mia and Jonas dragged them across the gravel one at a time. "I'm not allowed to climb," Jonas said, frowning up at the roof. "Then you can hold the bottom," said Mia. Jonas planted his feet and gripped the lowest crate with both hands. Mia set a second crate on top, then a third, leaning the pile against the church wall until its top touched the gutter. The pile wobbled, so Jonas pressed harder and it held. "I am going up," said Mia, and he put one boot on the first crate.

TEXT:
With three steps up the crates, the gutter came level with his hands. Mia pulled himself onto the roof, found his footing on the cold tiles, and climbed to the ridge. He stood up slowly with his arms out wide. "Careful," he said, and he walked along the top of the roof one step at a time. Below him, Jonas called his name. The parrot had caught in the gutter on the far side, its red ribbon hooked over a bracket. Mia crept along the ridge on his knees, leaned over the slope and reached. His fingers touched its soft green wing. Then another gust swept over the roof, the ribbon slipped free, and the parrot tumbled over the edge into the hedge behind the church.

The stack had stayed steady while he climbed, but as Mia stepped off the top crate, the crates slid apart on the wet leaves and tumbled across the gravel. Jonas had jumped back in time and stood unhurt beside the fallen crates. Mia flattened himself on the tiles and gripped the ridge with both hands. He peered over the edge. The wall below was bare, nothing left to climb, and the hedge hid the parrot. "Jonas, I am stuck," he called. Jonas looked up with his hands at his sides. "I cannot reach you," he said, and his voice came out small.
