--- Page 1 ---
TEXT:
Lily pulled her tall purple wizard hat down firmly and buckled her brown leather belt pouch. Hoot, her toy owl, was tucked under one arm with his wings folded close. Gran had said the old wizard at the top of Cobble Hill sat all alone with nobody to keep him company, so Lily had made up her mind to take Hoot up to him. She pushed the wardrobe door wide, squeezed past the old coats and stepped through into the green world on the other side. Behind her the door clicked shut. Ahead, a grassy path ran down between hedges toward a hill with a stone path winding up it. "Come on, Hoot," she said, and she set off at a trot.

--- Page 2 ---
TEXT:
At the bottom of the meadow, the only lane up to Cobble Hill was packed with gnomes. Some carried baskets, some carried flags, and one carried a long wooden spoon, but not one of them was moving. Lily stepped up to the nearest gnome. "Excuse me, please, I need to get past," she said. The gnome did not look round. Then a gnome called Bramble, standing at the lane's edge with his arms folded, said, "Lane's closed for the Harvest Parade. Nobody goes up Cobble Hill today." Lily looked at the crowd, then down at Hoot. Gran had always said gnomes could never resist a good rhyme, so Lily set her hat straight and began to think of one.

--- Page 3 ---
TEXT:
Climbing onto an old barrel by the stone wall, Lily lifted Hoot high and waved his wing. She sang in her loudest voice. "Gnomes in the lane, you're standing so still, step to the side and let us climb the hill! Hoot can flutter and I can sing, the silliest wizard in everything!" Hoot's wings flapped as she swayed his arm up and down. One gnome giggled. Then the whole crowd laughed, clapped and stepped aside, opening a gap wide enough for her to see the path. Bramble pushed through the gap, reached up with both hands and snatched Hoot away. "Not so fast," he said, holding the owl over his head. "He stays with me until the parade is done."

Bramble tucked Hoot under his own arm and marched off down the lane, and the gnomes closed up behind him. Lily climbed down from the barrel and sat on the low stone wall. Her arm stayed bent in the shape of the owl, and she held it against her side. Far down the meadow, Bramble's green coat disappeared behind the trees, and the lane stayed full. Lily tried the nearest gnome again, then the next, but nobody moved. She looked up the hill to the top, where the old wizard was waiting alone.

TEXT:
Back up on the barrel, Lily took a deep breath and pointed down the lane. "Bramble, Bramble, hear my song, you can't keep Hoot the whole day long!" Bramble came marching back up the lane with the owl under his arm, scowling. Behind him the gnomes began to giggle, then to laugh, and one of them clapped. Bramble stopped at the foot of the barrel and looked up. A laugh burst out of him before he could stop it. He held Hoot out at arm's length, and Lily leaned down from the barrel and took the owl back. The gnomes stepped aside, and the lane lay open all the way to the hill.

--- Page 6 ---
TEXT:
At the top of Cobble Hill, an old wizard sat on a stone bench with a cold cup of tea beside him. Lily walked up the last stretch of path and held out the toy owl. "This is Hoot," she said. "He is good company." The old wizard reached out with both hands and took Hoot, and then he smiled, slowly at first and then all the way across his face. "Thank you," he said softly. Lily reached out and smoothed one of Hoot's wings flat, then straightened her hat. "Goodbye, Hoot," she said, and she walked back down the hill with her chin held high.
