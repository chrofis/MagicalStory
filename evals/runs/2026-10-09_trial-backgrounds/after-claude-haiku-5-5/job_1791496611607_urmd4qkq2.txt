--- Page 1 ---
TEXT:
Pippa pulled hard on the lead, nose down, sniffing every wet leaf along the lane. Lily leaned back, dug her heels in and held on with both hands. Inside her canvas bag, the biscuit tin of treats knocked against her hip with every step. Pippa always stopped when that tin rattled. Ahead, the white tower of Kirche Rohrdorf rose above the hedges, and the lane flattened out near the top of the hill. Then a strong gust of wind swept up the slope. It tore the lead out of Lily's fingers. Pippa gave a joyful bark and shot off down the hill, the lead snaking behind her across the wet stones.

--- Page 2 ---

Calling Pippa's name, Lily ran down the hill after her. Pippa did not look back. Her tail streamed out and her paws kicked up orange leaves as she raced past the garden walls and a row of parked bicycles. Lily's shoes slapped on the stones, and her bag thumped against her side. The lead bounced over the leaves behind her. "Pippa, stop!" she shouted, but the lane only carried her voice back up the hill. At the bottom, Pippa swung round the corner and vanished from sight. Lily ran harder, her breath coming fast, and reached the bend just a moment later.

At the bottom of the lane, three narrow paths met beside a low stone wall. Lily reached the wall out of breath. Pippa was nowhere to be seen. Lily ran to the first path and looked along it, then to the second, then to the third. No dog came trotting back, and the only sound was a car passing on the road beyond the wall. Lily sat down on the wall with the bag in her lap and stared at the road. She called Pippa's name twice more. Nothing answered, and no ears appeared round the hedge. Her shoes scuffed the wet stone.

--- Page 4 ---
TEXT:
After a moment, Lily opened the bag and took out the biscuit tin. She stood up beside the wall, held the tin at arm's length and shook it hard. The treats rattled loudly against the metal. Lily shook it again, longer this time, and kept her eyes on the middle path. Then she walked to the corner where the paths met and shook it once more. Far up the middle path, a bark rang out, but no dog came into view. Lily's arm ached from the shaking, but she did not stop. Her bag lay open on the wall.

"Come on, Pippa," Lily called, and she climbed the middle path, shaking the tin at every corner. At the first bend nothing happened. At the second bend she shook it harder. The tin rattled again in Lily's hand, and the sound carried up the path. Far above, Pippa stood beside a garden gate, and at the sound she turned round. She came bounding down the lane with her ears flying and leapt straight into Lily's arms. Lily caught her, then held the trailing lead tight. Lily had kept her eyes on the gate the whole climb.

At the top of the path, the white church tower stood against the sky. Lily walked up the last stretch with Pippa trotting at her side. She held the lead in both hands and did not let it slip, even when a gust pushed against her. At the church gate, she stopped and turned to face the steps. "Good girl, Pippa," she said, and Pippa sat down beside her leg. Pippa's tail swept the gravel as she settled. Lily stood a little taller. Lily kept the lead tight as they climbed the church steps together.
