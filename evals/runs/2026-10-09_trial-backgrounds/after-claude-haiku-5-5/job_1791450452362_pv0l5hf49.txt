--- Page 1 ---
TEXT:
Ethan pounded up the stony path towards Ruine Stein, the gravel crunching under his trainers. The tower stood high above him on the hill, and a red and white flag fluttered at the very top. Then a grey goose stepped out of the bushes and stood right in the middle of the path. Ethan skidded to a halt.

"Move, goose!" he shouted. "I need to get to the top!"

The goose spread its wings wide and hissed at him. Ethan took one step back, then he shouted again, even louder. The goose hissed back louder still, and it did not move one step. Ethan planted his feet and frowned up at the flag. He wanted to reach the top now, and the goose was standing in the way.

--- Page 2 ---

"Ethan, you cannot shout a goose off a path," called Mum from the bottom of the steps. She climbed the last few stones and stopped beside him, breathing hard. "Let's go round it. There is another way through the trees."

"No, this way," Ethan said, cutting across her before she could finish. "The goose is in the way, so I want it to go now."

He stamped his trainer on the gravel. The goose hissed and took two steps towards him, and Ethan hopped back. Mum reached an open hand towards him, but he folded his arms and stared at the goose. "Then we will wait a little while," Mum said.

--- Page 3 ---
TEXT:
Beside the low wall, Ethan sat down on a flat stone and began to count the stones in it. He pointed at each one with a finger. "One, two, three," he said, loudly at first. The goose lifted its wings a little and then folded them again.

Ethan did not stop. Mum sat down beside him and counted along with him, her voice slower. "Ten, eleven, twelve," they said together. Ethan kept his finger moving along the wall, and the stones went on and on, all the way to the bushes. "Twenty," he said, and his legs kept wanting to jump up.

--- Page 4 ---
TEXT:
At twenty-six, Ethan could not wait any longer. He jumped up and ran at the goose with both arms out wide. The goose opened its wings, hissed hard and chased him back down the path. Ethan tripped on a root and landed on the gravel, his palms scraped and his knees on the stones.

When he got up, the goose was back in the middle of the path, pulling at a clump of grass. Ethan had lost his count in the rush, and he could not remember whether the last stone had been twenty-six or twenty-seven. Mum asked whether they should go home. Ethan did not answer, because the flag still looked very far away.

Ethan wiped his palms on his trousers and sat down on the flat stone again. He began at one. Mum sat beside him, and this time she waited for him to start. "One, two, three," Ethan said, pointing at each stone in turn, and he kept his voice steady as the numbers grew.

At thirty-nine he crouched by the last stone of the wall, ready to dash past the goose. His breath came fast, and his knees bent low. He stopped himself and said the number out loud. "Thirty-nine." Then he said, "Forty." The goose turned, walked slowly into the bushes and left the path clear. Ethan stood up and looked at the tower.

At the top of Ruine Stein, the stone steps ended beside the old tower. Ethan climbed the last steps and stood by the wall, with the flag fluttering high above him. Mum came up behind him, puffing. Below them the roofs of the town spread along the river, and a bridge crossed the water.

"Did you count all the way?" Mum asked.

"Every stone," said Ethan. He put his palm flat on the warm top of the wall and grinned at the view. "Forty stones, and then the top."
