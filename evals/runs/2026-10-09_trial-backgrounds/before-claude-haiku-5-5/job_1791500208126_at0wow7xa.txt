--- Page 1 ---
TEXT:
Lily pulled the wardrobe door wide and lifted her toy owl, Hoot, from the foot of her bed. Everyone in Fislisbach knew that the sad old wizard at the top of Cobble Hill had no one to keep him company since his own owl had flown away. Lily had decided he needed Hoot far more than she did. She took the purple robe from its hook and held it against her jumper, then reached up to the shelf for the tall hat with the golden band. "We are going to see him," she told Hoot, and she stood on her tiptoes to reach the hat.

--- Page 2 ---
TEXT:
Pulling the robe on over her jumper, Lily fastened the brown leather belt and tucked the little pouch in place. She set the tall purple hat on her head and tugged the wide brim straight. Then she tucked Hoot firmly under one arm and stepped through the coats at the back of the wardrobe. The coats brushed against her hat as she pushed through them. The soft carpet gave way to something hard and uneven under her feet. Lily had a plan, and it was a simple one. She would walk up the hill, hand Hoot to the wizard, and walk straight home again.

--- Page 3 ---
TEXT:
At the foot of Cobble Hill, a lane of cobblestones wound up between two grassy banks, with the green meadow falling away to the roofs of Fislisbach far below. The lane was full of gnomes. They stood shoulder to shoulder in red caps and brown, and not one of them stepped aside. Lily walked up to the back of the crowd. "Please, I need to get to the top of the hill," she said. A gnome standing on a wooden crate at the front folded his arms. "Grumbold says the lane stays shut," called a gnome beside her. Behind them, a gnome chanted a rhyme about pears and a sleepy bear, and his friends giggled.

--- Page 4 ---
TEXT:
Ducking low, Lily wriggled between two gnomes and made it halfway along the lane. Then a broad gnome shoulder met her, and another met her from the other side, and the crowd pressed in against her. Her hat tipped sideways over one eye. Hoot slipped from under her arm, and she caught him against her chest before he could hit the cobbles. "Please let me through," she said. Grumbold leaned out over his crate. "Back to the grass with you, little wizard." The gnomes pushed her out onto the bank beside a wooden barrel, and the gap closed behind her. The lane stayed shut.

--- Page 5 ---
TEXT:
Climbing onto the barrel, Lily stood tall above the crowd and held Hoot up on one arm. She remembered the rhyme about pears and the sleepy bear, and how every gnome had giggled at it. Gnomes loved a good rhyme. She took a deep breath and sang out in her loudest voice, "Flap, flap, Hoot, and wiggle your wing, gnomes in a crowd, now dance in a ring!" She jiggled his arm so his wings flapped at the front row. "Hop to the left, and hop to the right, open a lane for a wizard's delight!" One gnome snorted with laughter, then another, and soon the whole front row was bouncing on their toes. They clapped in time to the rhyme, and the gnomes shuffled apart until a lane lay open up the hill.

--- Page 6 ---
TEXT:
At the top of Cobble Hill, an old wizard sat on a low stone wall, with the roofs of Fislisbach spread across the green valley below him. He looked up when Lily came puffing over the last of the cobbles. She held Hoot out in both hands, the way she had carried him all the way up. The wizard took the owl slowly, turned him towards the light, and smiled, the first smile Lily had seen on him. Lily gave Hoot one last flap of his wing. "Look after him," she said. Then she set her hat straight and walked home down the lane.
