--- Page 1 ---
TEXT:
Mia raced down the meadow path with Captain Pip tucked under one arm and a coil of rope bouncing on his belt. Below him, the village of Fislisbach spread across the green valley, with white houses and red roofs. For a whole week, Mia had seen a boy sitting alone on the wall of the church, watching other children kick a ball. Today the boy was there again. Mia reached the bottom of the path, took a deep breath and called across the grass, "Ahoy, Jonas! I have got a present for you!" Jonas looked up. Mia lifted the parrot high so that his bright feathers caught the sun.

Beside the church, Mia held out Captain Pip, and Jonas stepped close to take him. A gust of wind tugged the parrot from Mia's fingers and carried him up over the roof. The roof rose three times Mia's height above the gravel. Captain Pip sailed over the ridge and caught on an iron hook at the top of the far gable. Mia ran round the side of the church, but the far wall had only a steep slope of tiles and a long drop to the path. "He is up there now," Jonas said quietly.

By the side door of the church hall, Mia found a stack of old wooden crates. "We will make a ladder," he said, and piled them against the church wall. Jonas held the bottom crate with both hands while Mia set the next one on top. Mia said he would test every crate first, and he pressed down hard on each one before stepping up. Then he climbed, one foot at a time, until he could grip the edge of the gutter. "Hold on tight, Jonas," he called down.

One boot slipped on a wet tile, so he dropped to one knee and waited until it steadied. Then he walked along the ridge, placing each boot with care. At the far gable, Captain Pip hung from the iron hook by his loop of string. Mia lifted the parrot free and pressed him against his chest with one hand, keeping the other arm out for balance.

Below him, the crates lay scattered across the gravel. When Mia stepped off the top crate, the bottom one had slid on the wet grass and the whole stack had tipped over. Jonas stood among the crates and looked up. "I cannot reach you," he called. Mia looked down at the long drop to the path. There was no ladder left, and the wall was too smooth to climb. "I am stuck up here," he said, and held Captain Pip tight against his vest.

Mia remembered the coil of rope on his belt. He unhooked it, looped one end twice round the iron hook and pulled the knot tight. Then he tied Captain Pip to the other end. "Jonas, catch!" he called, and let the rope down over the edge. Jonas caught it, lifted the parrot off the end and held the rope steady for Mia. Mia gripped the rope and walked down the wall one careful step at a time. "Go slowly," Jonas called. When Mia's boots touched the grass, Jonas held Captain Pip up high. "Welcome aboard the crew, Jonas," Mia said.
