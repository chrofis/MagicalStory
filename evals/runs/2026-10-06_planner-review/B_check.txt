# THE STORY

1. On an autumn afternoon Levin, his little brother Julian and their friends Max and Kiaan play ball on the Lindenhof, where the sun still shines on the Grossmünster towers across the roofs, and Kiaan wears his thick warm autumn jacket.
2. A man in an orange vest pushes his cart up onto the Lindenhof and starts his leaf blower, because he must get every leaf into the cart by the chess field before dark, so that nobody slips on them tomorrow.
3. When the ball rolls into the big leaf heap between the roots of a linden, Julian digs for it and finds an egg as big as a football and as warm as an oven tile, knocking softly from inside.
4. Nobody knows whose egg it is, but Julian spots a golden eye blinking in the linden's crown, and there, in her nest high in the branches, crouches a dragon as big as a horse, red and yellow exactly like the leaves.
5. Levin, who loves dragons, tells her their names, and she says she is Rubina: the cold wind from the lake shook her egg out of that nest, and it must be back in the warm nest before night, or her little one will never hatch.
6. But the linden is far too high for the children to climb, and Rubina cannot come down to them, because the roar hurts her fine ears, which are made to hear the faint knocking of her egg from far away, and with her wings pressed over her ears she cannot fly.
7. So the children pile leaves over the egg as a blanket, and Levin goes to ask the man to stop, but the blower swallows his whisper, and when the man switches off, says he is Herr Keller and asks "What?", Levin turns red and says nothing.
8. In that short quiet Julian sees Rubina climb down the trunk with her branch-claws toward the egg, but when Max shouts into Herr Keller's ear that a dragon egg lies under the leaves, Herr Keller laughs that dragons live only in books and switches on again, and Rubina climbs back up into the crown.
9. The blast blows the leaf blanket away, so the egg wobbles out of the roots and rolls down the slope toward the steep stairs.
10. Levin shouts "Careful, stairs!", and Max runs as fast as he can and stops the egg with his foot like a football, right at the top step.
11. Kiaan pulls off his warm jacket, wraps the egg in it and carries it carefully back under the linden, but the wind is cold and the knocking grows quieter.
12. Soon the sun lights only the tips of the Grossmünster towers, Herr Keller roars up to blow away the last leaves right under the linden, and inside the jacket the knocking almost stops.
13. But Julian remembers: "When it was quiet, Rubina came down!"
14. Levin saw that Herr Keller switches off for anyone who tugs his sleeve, so with shaking knees he tugs it and asks loudly how the leaf blower works.
15. Herr Keller switches off and turns to show him the cart by the chess field, his back to the linden, and Levin asks question after question: twice Herr Keller reaches for the switch, and twice Levin asks something new.
16. Rubina climbs down the trunk, a branch cracks and Herr Keller half turns, so she presses flat against the bark until Levin quickly asks how heavy the full cart is, and then she climbs the last bit and holds out claws that are made for branches and cannot grip the egg off the ground, so Kiaan lifts it up to her in his jacket.
17. She breathes warm air over the egg and carries it up into the nest, leaving the jacket in Kiaan's hands, and her wing beats shake every last leaf off the linden, so that Herr Keller turns round and sees his clean square covered red and yellow in the dusk and groans that he must start all over tomorrow.
18. Up in the warm nest the egg cracks and a little dragon peeks out at the four friends, and Rubina climbs down once more, says "Thank you!" and breathes a warm puff onto their cold hands, Julian strokes her nose, and Levin beams: "This time I dared!"
