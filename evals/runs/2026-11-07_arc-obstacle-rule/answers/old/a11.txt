[MAJOR] (s3, s5, s7) PLACE: The story cannot say whether the wet heap lies inside the roofed bridge or outside it, so the dry/wet contrast that carries the turn contradicts itself: Picot drags Bouton under a wet heap "at the far end of the bridge", where Léa stands inside and where Henri's roof keeps everything dry, yet the beam later falls on a "soaked heap outside" while the dry leaves lie in a corner inside, and Léa lays her boot "by the heap" — "Picot drags him under the wet leaf heap at the far end of the bridge" … "onto the dry leaves in the corner inside the bridge and onto the soaked heap outside". Smallest change: place the wet heap outside the arch in s5 (the Facts line "A roof keeps the leaves under it dry" already gives the inside).
2. [MAJOR] (s3) LOGIC: Henri's motive is that Léa be safe and in bed, yet he sends a four-year-old who has just said she is afraid of the dark alone across a wet night garden to a hedgehog, and stays at the door, although "too tall for the bridge" does not stop him walking her to the arch — "Léa is afraid of the dark garden and asks Henri to come, but Henri is too tall to fit through the low bridge" … "hands her the red boots and stays at the door". Smallest change: point to the Motives line "Henri: wants Léa safe" and have him walk her to the arch and wait outside it.
3. [MINOR] (s2) SENSE: Jules sees a hedgehog dragging Bouton "across the bridge" from the house window, though the bridge is roofed and walled with only two round windows, and the lamp cord reaches only the window — "Jules holds the lamp at the window, and its cord reaches only that far, so he shines it over the garden and sees a hedgehog dragging Bouton across the bridge".
